Why I Keep Writing to You (and What’s Coming)

You’ve been hearing from me a lot lately, and you deserve to know why.

I teach a Sunday school class. Every week I watch teenagers who are hungry for meaning and wholeness, and many of them feel unheard. I believe Christian Science has what they’re looking for, but it doesn’t always reach them in a form they can engage with. I can’t stay quiet about that. Not because I’m special, but because I’ve been given a great deal, and silence would feel dishonest.

Many young people say they don’t like the starting point they’ve been handed. They want a different one, and that’s what Christian Science offers. But it can be hard for them to engage with. Mary Baker Eddy’s writings are very absolute in places and relative in others, which is nuanced and confusing for many of us, never mind a teenager. I don’t think we’re reaching them as well as I’d like, and that deserves a loving, honest look, starting with ourselves.

So over the next nine days, I’ll share three short posts about two things we often hesitate to say out loud: how we talk about our shrinking numbers, and how we show human compassion. I write as a fellow traveler. I feel this too.

For the skeptics among us: no platitudes. We’ll look at those two fears through three lenses: the gap between what we say and what we do, the commitments we hold without naming them, and our own nostalgia for how things used to be. Each comes with claims you can test against your own experience.

Where I stand

I’m not a critic or a detractor. I love Christian Science. I’m a member of the Mother Church and class taught, and what I write is rooted in the Bible and Science and Health, and I aim to stay grounded in the Manual. I won’t get it perfect. I’m a learner, and I welcome being shown where I’ve missed. I don’t speak for the Church. I’m one loving Christian Scientist, following what I feel led to do. Honest questions, asked in love, move us forward. Uncomfortable doesn’t mean unloving.

I’d love your thoughts, whatever they are. I’m glad you’re here.
