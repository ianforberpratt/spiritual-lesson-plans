Why I Keep Writing to You (and What’s Coming)

You’ve been hearing from me a lot lately, and you deserve to know why.

I teach a Sunday school class. Every week I see teenagers who are hungry to know what their lives mean and how to feel whole, and many of them feel nobody is really listening. I believe Christian Science has what they’re looking for, but it often doesn’t reach them in words they can use. I’m writing because I’ve been given a great deal, not because I’m special.

Many young people say they don’t like the starting point they’ve been handed: a world that begins with the problem and asks them to fix it. They want a different one, and that’s what Christian Science offers. It begins with God and good. Mary Baker Eddy is clear that the absolute is the only reality. She is just as clear that most of us aren’t there yet. “The fact is, food does not affect the absolute Life of man,” she writes, and then: “it would be foolish to venture beyond our present understanding, foolish to stop eating until we gain perfection and a clear comprehension of the living Spirit” (Science and Health, p. 388). That isn’t two realities. It’s one reality and an honest path toward it. Holding both can be confusing for many of us, never mind a teenager, and it can feel risky, as if acknowledging where we are compromises the absolute. She treats it as wisdom, not compromise. I want to dig into that honestly, with you. Right now, I don’t think we’re reaching them as well as I’d like. That deserves a loving, honest look, starting with ourselves.

So over the next eight days, I’ll share three short posts about two things many of us find hard to say out loud: how we talk about our shrinking numbers (fewer people in our churches and Sunday schools), and how we show human compassion to people who are hurting. I write as a fellow traveler. I feel this too. Every step of this is prayerful.

For the skeptics among us: no platitudes. Each post takes one angle: the gap between what we say and what we do, the commitments we hold without naming them, and our own nostalgia for how things used to be. Each comes with claims you can test against your own experience.

What’s coming, at 6 PM Central

Sunday, November 22 — When ‘That Never Happened’ Meets a Hurting Teenager
“That never happened” comes from a fundamentally different view of what’s real, one that starts with God and good instead of with the problem. But a hurting teenager can hear it as “what you feel doesn’t count.” Some of us worry that caring about the pain in the moment compromises the absolute. This post wrestles with that honestly: can we hold the bigger view of reality and real care for someone right now?
Wednesday, November 25 — What We’re Quietly Protecting
Many of us follow some unspoken rules: don’t sound like you doubt, don’t admit when something didn’t work, don’t talk about the people who have left. They come from love and loyalty. This post says them out loud, so we can ask whether they’re protecting what matters or keeping young people out, and why our shrinking numbers are so hard to talk about.
Saturday, November 28 — The Church We Remember
Many of us miss the full Sunday schools and busy churches we grew up in. That longing comes from love. This post asks gently what it can do to how we see the young people who are here now, and what we might notice if we looked at them with fresh eyes.

Everything gathers in one place so you can catch up or read it all together.

Where I stand

I’m not a critic or a detractor. I love Christian Science. I’m a member of the Mother Church and class taught, and what I write is rooted in the Bible and Science and Health, and I aim to stay grounded in the Manual. I won’t get it perfect. I’m a learner, and I welcome being shown where I’ve missed. I don’t speak for the Church. I’m one loving Christian Scientist, following what I feel led to do. Honest questions, asked in love, move us forward. Uncomfortable doesn’t mean unloving.

I’d love your thoughts, whatever they are. I’m glad you’re here.
