Alongside

Mental health, human and spiritual care

Mental health is hard. For almost everyone, at some point. If you are struggling, or someone you love is, you are not alone, and you are not failing. This is a short, guided practice for anyone in the world: four ways to show up for a friend and for yourself, with human actions and spiritual ones side by side, drawing on wisdom from many traditions and from none.

You do not have to believe anything in particular to use it. Pick the words that feel like yours. Try the 5-minute version, do the full practice, or run it with a group. Nothing you type is saved or sent, and you can share it with anyone you love.

If you only have a minute

  1. Struggling is human. It is not a flaw, and it is not a verdict on you.
  2. It coming back is not failing. However many times a hard thing returns, it does not change your worth.
  3. Tell one person. You do not need the right words. “This has been heavy” is enough.
  4. Make the load smaller. One small step today: water, a walk, a breath, a prayer, one easy task.
  5. Be still for a minute. Not to solve anything. Just to rest in whatever you hold sacred.
  6. Use every kind of help. Spiritual care and human care work together: friends, family, doctors, counselors, and your own faith or values.

If you are in danger right now: in the US, call or text 988 any hour, or call 911. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number or look for a helpline in your country (findahelpline.com).

For teachers, youth groups, support groups, and teams

How to run it

  • For a group, it works best in crews of 3 or 4, each person on their own phone, sitting together. The private parts (how are you doing, who could you tell) stay on each person’s own screen. Read-aloud parts are read quietly to the crew.
  • Plan on about 35 minutes. A second “friend” is optional and adds points.
  • Each crew picks one friend to practice with, so they can compare answers. Different crews can pick different friends. “You, overwhelmed” is the personal one. Eli’s story is heavier and is easy to skip.

The Crew Words

Students pause and reflect until you say the next word aloud. Say them when everyone has finished each part:

  • Warm-up: banana
  • After Notice: pickle
  • After Listen: llama
  • After Loosen: waffle
  • After Loop in: hiccup
  • If one student is stuck, quietly tell them openup.

Faith-neutral by design

Students choose their own words for what they hold sacred (God, the Divine, Spirit, Love, the Universe, or what I hold sacred), and the practice uses those words. The sayings and scriptures keep their own wording. You are welcome to invite students to bring a saying from their own tradition or their own values, and to make clear that no one has to share.

Safety

  • Some of this touches hard things, including a scenario about a friend who may be thinking about self-harm. Read it yourself first.
  • If a student tells you they are struggling, stay with them, tell a trusted adult the same day, and follow your organization’s safeguarding policy. In the US, 988 is call or text, any hour; 911 for danger right now. Outside the US, use your local crisis line.
  • This is a practice, not treatment. It does not replace a doctor, counselor, or crisis service.

Scripture quotations marked KJV are from the King James Version. The Quran is quoted from the Sahih International translation. Other passages are from Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures by Mary Baker Eddy. The story of Kisa Gotami is retold from the Buddhist tradition, and tellings differ in small details. The ideas from Hindu, Sikh, and Buddhist traditions, and from people with no religious belief, are summaries, not quotations. Each person is the best guide to their own tradition.
