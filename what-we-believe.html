What We Believe

Where these lessons come from

This isn't an institution, and there's nothing to join. Here's a plain-language look at the ideas underneath every lesson on this site.

Our core approach

A few threads you'll see again and again

01

You are already whole

Not broken and in need of fixing. Not incomplete until you achieve something. Whole now, as a starting point — not a finish line.

02

Love isn't conditional

God's love, as Christian Science describes it, doesn't fluctuate with behavior, doubt, or belief. That's the model every lesson tries to offer a young person, too.

03

Healing is your right

Not something you have to earn, or hope you're lucky enough to receive. The universe already sees you as whole — healing is just the journey of coming to understand that completeness.

The Foundations

Where this actually comes from

The three ideas above didn't come from nowhere, and we think you deserve the real story behind them — plainly, whether Christian Science is a name you already know or one you're hearing for the first time right now.

In short: it's a 150-year-old Christian tradition built on the idea that everyone is already whole, that God's love doesn't have to be earned, and that healing is a right — not something you have to earn or hope you're lucky enough to receive. It isn't a cult, isn't a secret, and isn't exclusive — it started as one woman's serious, decades-long study of the Bible, published for anyone to read, and it's still practiced by real people today.

Your own faith is welcome here, and it'll likely match this in plenty of places. Where it doesn't, that's really what this whole site is about: taking what's useful from more than one place, and letting our different ways of seeing things help us care for each other instead of pulling us apart.

Expand whichever of these interest you — including honest answers to the questions people sometimes bring to something like this. If another question comes up that isn't here, ask it anytime — the feedback button below is always open.

A woman, a fall on the ice, and a question that stuck

In 1866, a woman named Mary Baker Eddy was badly injured in a fall. While she was recovering, she read an account of one of Jesus's healings — and found herself well. That moment sent her back into the Bible for nine years, studying how Jesus healed and taught, and trying to understand what made it work.

What came out of that study was a book, Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures, published in 1875. In 1879 she founded a church to teach it: the Church of Christ, Scientist. She called what she'd found "Christian Science" — Christian, because she believed she was pointing back to how Jesus actually taught; Science, because she believed it was a provable, practical law, not a private opinion.

That's it. No mystery, no secret text, no hidden layer. One woman's serious, decades-long study of the Bible, published for anyone to read.

The old way and the new way

In plain terms, here's the shift at the center of these lessons.

The old way: God as a distant, score-keeping judge. Love you have to earn. Prayer as a request you send up and hope gets approved. Sickness, fear, and failure as just "how life is."

The new way — which Eddy argued was actually the older, truer way, the one Jesus taught before centuries of institutions layered fear on top of it: God as Love itself, already present, not fluctuating with your behavior. Prayer as aligning your thinking with what's already true, not a request into the void. And the conviction that identity, wholeness, and healing aren't things you achieve — they're things you already have, underneath whatever's covering them up.

That's the "you are already whole" premise this whole site is built on. It didn't start with this site. It's about 150 years old.

What if I don't believe in Jesus?

That's completely fine, and it doesn't change how welcome you are here. We use Jesus's example the way you'd use any powerful story that inspires you — not as a God you're being asked to adopt. On this site, "God" just means a power greater than ourselves, however you understand that, and we don't call Jesus, or anyone else, that.

You'll see Jesus mentioned again and again in these lessons. That's not a quiet ask to believe something new. It's because his example — of love, courage, and treating people like they're already whole — is one of the clearest ones we know of. Take the story, leave the theology if that's not yours. Your faith is so welcome here, exactly as it is.

And the medical question, plainly

This is the part that deserves the most directness, so here it is: historically, Christian Science has taught that prayer alone — without medical treatment — is the more spiritually complete way to seek healing. Many practitioners of Christian Science today still choose that path for themselves, as adults, by their own informed choice. That's a real, live practice, and it's not this site's place to tell you what to think of it.

What we want to be completely clear about: nothing in these lessons is medical guidance, and nothing here ever tells a young person — or a mentor — to delay, avoid, or replace medical or mental health care with prayer. If a young person in your group is sick, hurting, in crisis, or needs a doctor or a counselor, get them one. That's not a failure of faith. These lessons are about identity, belonging, and how someone thinks about themselves and a power greater than them — not about medical decisions, and never will be.

For the fuller, official answer straight from the church, see “How does a Christian Scientist approach health and healing?” on the Christian Science FAQ.

"Isn't that the same as Scientology?"

No — different founders, different centuries, different everything, and the confusion is understandable but the two have no real connection.

Christian Science was founded by Mary Baker Eddy in 1879, a Christian denomination built on God, Jesus, and the Bible. Scientology showed up decades later, founded by L. Ron Hubbard in 1953-54, with its own framework and vocabulary entirely — ideas like the "thetan" that have nothing to do with anything here. Really, the two just happen to share one English word. Different founders, different centuries, different everything else.

The fuller history, including the parts that are hard to hear

Everything above is the whole picture as far as this site is concerned. But if you want the deeper history — including the parts that don't reflect well on the movement — you deserve the real version, not a defensive one, so here it is.

Christian Science has been called a cult, and it's fair to ask why. A few real reasons: Mary Baker Eddy led the church with a lot of personal authority during her lifetime, and some of her own writing about opponents and rivals — she used a term, "malicious animal magnetism," to describe what she believed was harmful mental influence directed at her — reads, by today's standards, as genuinely strange and a little paranoid. The church was insular for much of its history. And most seriously: because Christian Science has historically emphasized prayer over medical treatment, there are published cases of people facing serious medical struggles, and even death, under prayer-only treatment. Medical treatment carries its own real risks and failures too — people are harmed and people die under medical care as well. Neither fact cancels out the other. It's genuinely messy, and it deserves to be looked at honestly from every side, not just whichever side is easiest to defend.

We're not going to pretend that history isn't there. It is, and it matters.

Here's what's also true: this site is not the Church of Christ, Scientist. Nobody here is asking a young person to join anything, follow a leader, or choose prayer over a doctor. Nothing on this site ever will. What we've taken from Christian Science is the lens — the idea that a young person is already whole, that love isn't conditional, that healing is a right, not a reward. You're free to use every lesson here and never think about Mary Baker Eddy again.

Every faith background welcome

You don't have to believe any of this to use these lessons

Whatever tradition you or the young people you mentor come from, these ideas are offered as a lens, not a requirement. When a lesson cites a source, it's usually Science and Health or the Bible — not because those are the only valid ones, but because they're this team's own lens, and you're free to swap in a story or verse from your own if it says it better. That said, we do hope you'll consider the core approach here too — we believe it genuinely makes a difference. Take what's useful, leave what isn't, and adapt freely.
