## What you should know

This age can hold the full paradox: social media has built the most comparison-saturated
environment teenagers have ever lived inside, and it's exactly why this distinction lands so
hard here. Keep examples anchored in independence and identity actively forming — a flopped
post, a group chat, a missed goal, comparing yourself to someone's highlight reel — not childhood
scenarios and not fully adult ones (workplace, marriage) either. Near-adult discussion depth is
appropriate; you don't need to soften the discomfort of naming how much of daily life runs on the
little-e ego.

**What we're assuming**

- Social media use is close to universal at this age, and this lesson names it directly rather
  than talking around it.
- Some in the room may currently be mid-comparison-spiral — a breakup, a rejection, a social
  flop. Hold that gently; this lesson can land as relief or as a fresh sting, depending on timing.
- Real discussion is appropriate here, but still offer an explicit way to pass on any activity.

## Learning goals

1. Notice the little-e ego's specific voice in their own recent scrolling or comparing.
2. Feel the real difference between defending an identity and resting in one already whole.
3. Carry a concrete practice into a real moment this week.
4. Locate one current relationship or situation — a comparison, a grudge, a fear of missing out —
   to apply it to.

## How to hold the room

- Near-adult discussion depth is fine here. You're not there to resolve the discomfort of naming
  how much of daily life runs on the little-e ego — sit in it.
- Offer a real opt-out on any activity, especially the closing practice.
- Some pushback is likely and worth welcoming — "you don't need approval" can sound naive against
  how real social consequences actually feel at this age. Don't argue it away; hold both truths.

## The lesson flow

### Beat 1 (~5 min) — Opening hook

"When someone says you have a big ego, that's an insult. What if I told you God actually wants
you to have a big Ego — just not the one you think?" Let it sit before explaining.

### Beat 2 (~7 min) — Word study

"Ego" is Latin for "I." Read together:

> "I, or EGO. Divine Principle; Spirit; Soul; incorporeal, unerring, immortal, and eternal Mind.
> There is but one I, or Us, but one divine Principle, or Mind, governing all existence... All
> the objects of God's creation reflect one Mind..." — Science and Health with Key to the
> Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 588:9–19

> "And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM." — Exodus 3:14

Ask: "Whose 'I' is actually running the show in most of your thoughts on an average day?"

### Beat 3 (~8 min) — Naming the little-e ego honestly

Read:

> "The material body, which you call me, is mortal mind, and this mind is material in sensation."
> — Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 416:16

Unpack plainly: the "I" most people live inside — anxious, competitive, keeping score — isn't a
lowercase, humbler version of the real you. It's a different, borrowed thing entirely, built out
of comparison and material sensation, and it was never equipped to generate real security to
begin with. That's why it's exhausting.

### Beat 4 (~10 min) — Core exercise (pick one)

**The mirror exercise.** Hold up an actual mirror. Ask what it shows. Connect it to the idea that
your true selfhood isn't a self-generating reflector with its own agenda — it's a reflection,
like a mirror reflects light it didn't create. Ask: "What changes if I think of myself as
reflecting Ego rather than being a separate, self-made little ego?"

**The two-selves T-chart.** Fold paper in half. One side: everything little-e ego says (I'm not
enough, what do they think of me, comparison, fear, needing likes or approval, grudges, pride).
Other side: what big-E Ego knows to be true (complete, whole, loved, unafraid, God's own idea).
Individual first, then optional pairs.

### Beat 5 (~12 min) — Scenario / small-group discussion

Give scenarios pulled straight from their world: getting cut from a team, a post that flops,
being left out of a group chat, comparing yourself to someone's highlight reel, losing an
argument with a parent.

Small groups: "What's little-e ego's reaction here? What does big-E Ego already know that makes
that reaction unnecessary?"

### Beat 6 (~8 min) — Closing practice (pick one)

**Sticky-note release.** Each person writes one little-e thought they're ready to let go of (a
fear, a comparison, a grudge) on a sticky note, then physically discards it — crumple, toss,
cross out.

**Spoken affirmation, round-robin.** Each person completes "I am..." with a big-E truth (whole,
loved, unafraid, God's own reflection) rather than a little-e label. Offer a full pass option —
no one has to speak.

**Closing**

"The version of you that has to keep proving itself was never actually you. You're allowed to
stop auditioning."

## What you'll need

- Paper and pens
- Sticky notes, if using that closing practice
- A mirror, if using that exercise
- Print and hand out the ["Big Ego, Little Ego"](/assets/materials/big-ego-little-ego/big-ego-little-ego-wallet-card.html) wallet card

## If it's not going well

Silence is normal — let it sit. Disagreement or pushback is welcome, not a problem, especially
around whether approval "actually" matters practically — it does, and that's not the point being
made. A strong emotional reaction, especially if a recent real rejection or loss is surfacing —
pause, ground the room, check in privately. A disclosure of real harm — self-harm, abuse, a
serious mental-health crisis — stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, and point to the
site's mentor safety guidelines and each facilitator's own local mandatory-reporting obligations.
