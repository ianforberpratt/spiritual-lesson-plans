# Lesson Core Model: "Big Ego, Little Ego"

*Source lesson: Ian's original idea on Ego (God, the one true "I") vs. ego (the mortal, material
sense of self) — capital-E vs. lowercase-e. This core model follows the same pattern established
for "Is God Keeping Score?": one spiritual core, independently-designed age-band deliveries. This
document is shared scaffolding; it is never taught directly.*

## Why only four bands, not five

The whole engine of this lesson is holding two senses of "I" apart at once — noticing that the
voice narrating your day ("I'm not enough," "what do they think of me") isn't automatically the
truth about you, and that there's a different, steadier "I" underneath it. That's a
meta-cognitive move — stepping outside your own thinking to examine it — and it isn't available
yet at 5-8, where a child's sense of self is still forming, not yet separable into "the voice I
have" versus "the voice I actually am." Building a 5-8 version would either water this down into
a generic "God loves you" lesson that duplicates ground already covered by *Is God Keeping
Score?* and *Trust the Mover*, or reach for a self-reflective capacity this age doesn't have.
Default to not building it, per the site's "fit what naturally works" rule.

**Bands built:** 8-11, 11-14, 14-21, 21-plus.

## The spiritual core (constant across all built bands)

**Central claim:** There is only one real "I" — one Ego, one divine Mind, one Principle governing
all existence — and every person's actual identity is that one Mind's individual, permanent
reflection, already whole. The "I" most people live inside day to day — anxious, competitive,
needing to win the room, win the argument, win the comparison — isn't a smaller, humbler version
of that true identity. It's a different thing entirely: a borrowed, material sense of self that
was never equipped to generate its own worth in the first place, which is exactly why it's so
tired.

**The picture being replaced:** the belief that "I" am a private operation — self-made,
self-running, responsible for building and defending my own worth, status, and safety — so that
every comparison, snub, loss, or criticism lands as a direct hit on who I actually am.

**The picture being offered:** identity as reflection, not invention. One Mind, one Ego, and
every person an individual, unchanging expression of it — "numbers which never blend with each
other, though they are governed by one Principle." A reflection isn't threatened by another
reflection nearby. It was never in the business of generating its own light to begin with.

**Anchor citations:**
- "I, or EGO. Divine Principle; Spirit; Soul; incorporeal, unerring, immortal, and eternal Mind.
  There is but one I, or Us, but one divine Principle, or Mind, governing all existence; man and
  woman unchanged forever in their individual characters, even as numbers which never blend with
  each other, though they are governed by one Principle. All the objects of God's creation
  reflect one Mind, and whatever reflects not this one Mind, is false and erroneous, even the
  belief that life, substance, and intelligence are both mental and material." — Science and
  Health with Key to the Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 588:9–19
- "The material body, which you call me, is mortal mind, and this mind is material in sensation,
  even as the body, which has originated from this material sense and been developed according to
  it, is material." — Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 416:16
  (under the subhead "The so-called physical ego")
- "And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of
  Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you." — Exodus 3:14, KJV

**Facilitator-only background, not for direct quoting with learners** (the citation isn't precise
enough to anchor a taught line the way the three above are): Eddy elsewhere distinguishes an
*egotist* (someone who talks a lot about himself) from *egoism*, "a passionate love of self, which
doubts all existence except its own" — and observes that evil is *egotistic*, boastful but
"fleeing like a shadow at daybreak," while God is *egoistic*, "knowing only His own all-presence,
all-knowledge, all-power" (*Unity of Good*, the section titled "The Ego"). Useful color for how
you personally think about the 21-plus material — the little ego performs because it isn't sure
it's real; the true Ego doesn't perform, because nothing about it is in question.

## Learning objective family

1. **Notice** the little-ego voice as it actually shows up — the specific thought, not just
   "feeling insecure" in the abstract.
2. **Feel**, not just intellectually agree, that this voice narrating the day isn't automatically
   the truth about who's there.
3. **Carry** one repeatable move — a question, a phrase, a practice — into a real moment this
   week.
4. **Locate** one real, current situation where the little-ego voice tends to show up loudest.

## The bridge, by age band

| Age band | What the tension looks like to them | Bridge used |
|---|---|---|
| 8-11 | Not yet ready for letter-case wordplay or self-narration as a concept — but very ready to compare two objects side by side | A flashlight (makes its own light, runs out of batteries, has to protect its charge) vs. a mirror in sunlight (never runs out, because it was never making the light to begin with — just reflecting what's already there) |
| 11-14 | Grades, group chats, being picked or not picked, comparison with friends — self-worth unusually externally anchored at this age | The letter-case idea becomes usable now: "big-E Ego" and "little-e ego" as two different voices, explored through a private T-chart before any sharing |
| 14-21 | Social media specifically, identity actively forming, near-adult stakes (a flopped post, a group chat, a missed goal, comparing to someone's highlight reel) | Same two-voices frame, deepened with real scenario work in small groups and a closing embodied practice (naming and releasing a little-ego thought, or a spoken "I am" round) |
| 21-plus | Career, marriage, parenting, civic life — professional ego, defensiveness in conflict, needing credit or needing to be right | Same frame applied to adult scenarios: a meeting where credit gets contested, a disagreement with a spouse, a moment of feeling threatened by someone else's success |

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **5-8 isn't built at all** — see above. Not a diluted version; no version.
- **Capital/lowercase letter wordplay as the literal teaching device** stays out of 8-11. That
  band gets the same insight through two objects (flashlight vs. mirror) rather than through
  reading "Ego" and "ego" as a distinction — asking an 8-year-old to hold a metalinguistic
  distinction on top of a self-reflective one is one abstraction too many. 11-14 and up can hold
  it directly.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion** stays out of 8-11 (simple paired comparison only, per the
  site's discussion-format rule for that band) and gets a private-reflection option first at
  11-14.
- **Workplace, marriage, parenting, and civic-life material** stays 21-plus only.
- **The "egotistic vs. egoistic" aside above** isn't quoted directly to learners at any band — the
  citation isn't precise enough to anchor a taught line the way the three primary citations are.
  Facilitator background only.
