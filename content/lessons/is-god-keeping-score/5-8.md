## What you should know

Kids this age think in pictures and stories, not ideas. They will not follow "God's nature is Love, not a reward for good behavior" as a sentence — but they will absolutely follow a story about a parent whose love doesn't change based on a messy room. Don't explain the concept; let the story carry it. Resist the urge to add an abstract wrap-up sentence at the end ("so you see, boys and girls, this means...") — if the story worked, they already have it, and a spoken-out-loud "moral" often flattens what they just felt.

Most 5–8 year-olds already know sticker charts, behavior clip-charts, or "good job" praise systems from school or home. That's not a bad thing to name — it's the perfect concrete hook, because they already know exactly how it feels when the sticker doesn't come.

## What we're assuming

- Every child in the room has had at least one day where they didn't "earn" praise, a sticker, or a treat — and felt something about that, even if they can't name it.
- This is not the moment to critique sticker charts or classroom behavior systems generally — teachers and parents use them for good reasons. The point is much narrower: God's love specifically isn't one of those systems.
- Some kids may ask "does God still love me when I'm bad?" out loud, mid-story. That's the lesson working. Answer simply and warmly: "Yes. Every single time. That never changes."

## Learning goals

1. A felt sense (not a spoken definition) that God's love doesn't depend on being "good."
2. One phrase they can say by heart: *"God's love doesn't go up or down. God's love just is."*
3. A picture they made themselves, to take home and see again.

## How to hold the room

- Keep it moving. This age group's attention holds for a story and a song, not for sitting still and discussing. If you feel the room getting restless, you're probably lingering on a beat too long — move to the next one.
- Use your own voice and face, not just words — this age reads tone before content.
- If a child shares something vulnerable ("I didn't get a sticker today and I felt sad"), receive it warmly in the moment, don't turn it into group discussion. A simple "I'm glad you told me that" is enough.

## The lesson flow

### Beat 1 (~3 min) — Gathering

Sit in a circle. Simple welcome: "I'm so glad you're here today. Every single one of you." (Say each child's name if the group is small enough — this age responds strongly to being named.)

### Beat 2 (~7 min) — The story

Tell this simply, in your own words, using a stuffed animal or puppet as the "child" character if you have one:

*"There once was a family with a kid who sometimes cleaned up their toys, and sometimes didn't. Sometimes said 'please' and 'thank you,' and sometimes forgot. And every single night, no matter what kind of day it had been, the mom or dad would tuck them in and say the exact same thing: 'I love you. I love you the same amount as yesterday, and the same amount as tomorrow.' Not more on the good days. Not less on the hard days. The same. Every night."*

Ask (simple, concrete, one at a time — not a discussion):

- "Do you know anyone in your life who loves you like that?"
- Let a few kids answer if they want to. No pressure.

### Beat 3 (~5 min) — Naming it as God

"That's what God's love is like for every single one of you. Not a sticker chart where good days earn more and bad days earn less. God's love doesn't go up. God's love doesn't go down. God's love just *is* — every day, the same, for you."

Read (in simple, warm voice, no need to explain the words):

> "The Lord your God is with you... He will take great delight in you... he will rejoice over you with singing." — Zephaniah 3:17

"God doesn't just tolerate you. God sings about you. Every day. Same amount."

### Beat 4 (~3 min) — Say it together

Teach the phrase with a simple motion (hands going flat/level, side to side, to show "doesn't go up or down"):

*"God's love doesn't go up or down. God's love just is."*

Say it together 2–3 times, with the motion, maybe a simple tune if you're comfortable singing.

### Beat 5 (~5–7 min) — Craft: take it home

Give each child paper and crayons/markers. Draw a simple sun (a circle with rays). In the middle, write or help them write "God loves me" — and on the rays, help them draw or write things about their day, good or hard ones mixed together, to show: the sun doesn't get bigger or smaller depending on what's written on the rays.

### Closing

Circle up. Say the phrase together one more time. Simple closing: "God loves you the exact same amount today as every day. See you next week."

## What you'll need

- Paper, crayons or markers, one per child
- A stuffed animal or simple puppet (optional, helps hold attention during the story)
- Nothing else — keep the room simple and uncluttered for this age

## If it's not going well

If a child discloses something that sounds like it's about real neglect, harm, or a caregiver who is genuinely inconsistent or frightening (not just an ordinary hard day), don't probe further in the moment — stay warm, say something like "thank you for telling me, I care about that," and follow the site's mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page) afterward, including notifying the appropriate adult per your church's child-safety policy. This age group sometimes discloses real concerns inside a story that feels safe to them — take it seriously even though it arrived gently.
