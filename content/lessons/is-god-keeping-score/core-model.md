# Lesson Core Model: "Is God Keeping Score?"

*This is the pilot for the site's age-band content pattern: one theological core, independently-designed
delivery layers per age. This document is shared scaffolding — it is never rendered directly to a
visitor. It's the thing every age version is checked against, so every band stays recognizably the
same truth even though the pages look nothing alike.*

## Why a "core + layers" model instead of one lesson translated several ways

Age-grading this content isn't a simplify-the-reading-level pass. A 7-year-old and a 25-year-old don't
need the same idea in smaller words — they need the idea delivered through completely different
cognitive doors, because concrete-operational thinking (roughly ages 5–11) and abstract thinking
(emerging 11–14, consolidating mid-teens) process metaphor, hypotheticals, and self-reflection
differently. Writing "one lesson, five reading levels" would fail the youngest bands. Writing "one
spiritual core, several independently-designed deliveries" is more work up front but is the only
version that actually respects where each age group is developmentally.

Practically: write this section once per lesson topic. Each age-band lesson document opens with a
pointer back here, then never re-litigates the theology — only the delivery changes.

## The spiritual core (constant across all ages)

**Central claim:** God's very nature *is* Love — not judgment first, love second, earned back through
good behavior. There is no ledger. There was never a scoreboard.

**The picture being replaced:** a punishing or scorekeeping God, where grace is conditional and every
failure moves a tally the wrong direction. This picture is often carried as "reverence" without being
named as fear.

**The picture being offered:** a God whose love doesn't fluctuate with performance — "ever beautiful
and complete," delighting in a person rather than grading them.

**Anchor citations (used at whatever reading level fits the age band):**
- "God is Love. Can we ask Him to be more?" — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 2:23
- "Man is God's reflection, needing no cultivation, but ever beautiful and complete." — Science and
  Health, p. 527:4
- "The Lord your God is with you, the Mighty Warrior who saves. He will take great delight in you; in
  his love he will no longer rebuke you, but will rejoice over you with singing." — Zephaniah 3:17, NIV
- "Divine Love always has met and always will meet every human need." — Science and Health, p. 494:10

## Learning objective family

Each age band aims at a version of the same four objectives — the verbs and depth shift, the target
doesn't:

1. **Notice** the difference between a scary/earned kind of "good behavior" and a secure/loved kind.
2. **Feel**, not just understand intellectually, that love isn't something to re-earn after a mistake.
3. **Carry** something concrete and repeatable — a phrase, a question, a picture — into a real moment
   this week.
4. **Locate** one real relationship or situation in their own life where this applies.

## The bridge metaphor, by age band

This is the one piece that's genuinely different per age, and it's the hinge the whole lesson turns
on — get this wrong and the lesson doesn't land, no matter how good the rest is.

| Age band | What "scorekeeping" looks like to them | Bridge metaphor used |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | Sticker charts, "good job" praise, being sent to time-out | A parent's love vs. a sticker chart |
| 8-11 | Game points, app streaks, grades starting to matter | Winning/losing a game vs. a friend who's there either way |
| 11-14 | Grades, social media likes, peer ranking, comparison | Being "graded" by others' approval vs. being fully known and still fully loved |
| 14-21 | **Not yet written — open gap, assigned to Ian.** Likely: first jobs, dating, leaving home, early independence | Suggested starting point: being "graded" on how well you're adulting vs. being fully loved mid-transition |
| 21+ | Performing for God, defending being "right" in conflict, workplace/marriage tension | The ledger-God vs. the differently-sized God |

## What does NOT get inherited across age bands

- **Discussion format.** Under-11s should not get open-ended abstract discussion questions — they don't
  have the cognitive tools yet to answer these in the way the question intends, and forcing it produces
  confused silence or rote mimicry rather than real understanding.
- **The geopolitical / workplace conflict examples.** These stay in the 21+ version only. Middle
  schoolers get relational examples they actually live in (friendship, family, social media); younger
  kids get none of the "applied to conflict" material at all.
- **Any content flagged sensitive.** Some topics don't get a diluted version for young ages — they get
  no version, by design, not by oversight.

## Facilitator-coaching principle

Below age 14 or so, assume the volunteer teacher has less training in open discussion facilitation
than the 21+ material assumes. Each younger-band lesson should coach the teacher in the moment ("if a
child asks X, here's a simple true answer"), not just hand over content.
