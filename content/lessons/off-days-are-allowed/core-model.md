# Lesson Core Model: "Off Days Are Allowed"

*One theological core, five possible age-specific deliveries (this topic uses four of the five bands — see below). Everything in this document is shared scaffolding — it does not get taught directly to any age group. It's the thing every age version is checked against, so the different lessons stay recognizably the same truth even though the room, the words, and the activities look nothing alike from one band to the next.*

Note on bands: this lesson is built for 5-8, 8-11, 11-14, 14-21, and 21+. All five genuinely fit — everyone, at every age, has off days, and the sneaky belief this lesson answers ("if I were doing this right, I wouldn't feel this way") shows up in some form as early as early elementary and stays live well into adulthood.

## Central claim

Feeling disconnected, low, or "off" is not proof of failure — spiritual, emotional, or otherwise. A person's wholeness isn't a balance that a bad day draws down. It was never something they had to earn moment to moment, so it isn't something an off day can take away.

## The picture being replaced

A performance-based picture of feeling okay: the sneaky belief that if you were "doing it right" — praying enough, trusting enough, being positive enough, grateful enough, put-together enough — you wouldn't feel disconnected or low. Under this picture, a hard feeling isn't just hard, it's also evidence against you. People carrying this belief often respond to a bad day by grading themselves on top of already feeling bad, which is a second, avoidable injury layered on the first, real one.

## The picture being offered

A hard truth and a hopeful truth can both be true in the same breath. "I know things are okay, but right now I feel scared" isn't a contradiction and isn't a failure of faith — it's just honest. An off day is information about today, not an indictment of who someone is or how well they're doing at being a person. Wholeness underneath doesn't fluctuate with mood, the way the sun doesn't stop being the sun on a cloudy day.

## Anchor citations

Used at whatever depth and reading level fits the age band — younger bands may only ever hear these read aloud without discussion, and that's enough.

- "God is Love. Can we ask Him to be more?" — *Science and Health*, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 2:23
- "Man is God's reflection, needing no cultivation, but ever beautiful and complete." — *Science and Health*, p. 527:4
- "Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning." — Psalm 30:5, KJV
- "Divine Love always has met and always will meet every human need." — *Science and Health*, p. 494:10

## Learning objective family

Each age band aims at a version of the same four objectives — the verbs and depth shift, the target doesn't:

1. **Notice** that an off day — feeling low, disconnected, more human than usual — is a real feeling, not proof of doing something wrong.
2. **Feel**, not just understand intellectually, the relief of not having to perform wellness or positivity on a day that's genuinely hard.
3. **Carry** a repeatable practice: naming a hard truth and a hopeful truth in the same breath, instead of picking one to fake.
4. **Locate** one real moment in their own life — this week or a recent one — where this applies.

## The bridge, by age band

This is the piece that's genuinely different per age, and it's the hinge the whole lesson turns on.

| Age band | What the core tension looks like concretely | Bridge used |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | A grumpy or sad day with no clear reason; worry that it makes them a "bad kid" | Even the sun has cloudy days — it's still the same sun underneath |
| 8-11 | Being able to name their own low or grumpy mood, and wondering if it means something's actually wrong with them | An athlete's off game — one bad game doesn't erase being good at the sport |
| 11-14 | Grades, social comparison, and the pressure to seem fine — the sneaky belief that a hard day is proof of "doing it wrong" | Grading yourself — naming the belief "if I were doing this right, I wouldn't feel this way" as the actual problem |
| 14-21 | Feeling disconnected or low and worrying it means they're failing at faith, positivity, or "having it together" | Being tested vs. being honest — practicing "I know ___ is true, but right now I feel ___" |
| 21+ | An off day at work while still expected to perform, as a parent, in a marriage, or under pressure to seem "fine" or "blessed" in front of others | The same honest-truth-and-hopeful-truth practice, aimed at the specific performance pressures of adult roles |

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **Open, abstract discussion questions.** 5-8 gets no discussion questions at all — only simple, concrete, one-at-a-time asks ("has anyone felt grumpy today, even for no reason?"). Concrete-operational thinkers at this age can't yet reflect on an abstract belief like "grading yourself," so asking them to produces confused silence or rote mimicry, not real understanding.
- **The "I know ___ is true, but right now I feel ___" verbal practice.** This stays out of 5-8 entirely (it's a piece of abstract self-reflection language they're not ready to produce) and appears only in a simplified, non-required form at 8-11. It becomes a real, practiced tool starting at 11-14.
- **Private journaling before sharing.** This beat starts at 11-14, where self-consciousness about being watched while vulnerable becomes intense enough to need it. Younger bands don't need it (they share more freely and with less at stake); it's assumed and lighter-touch by 21+, where adults are generally comfortable choosing whether to share without being prompted to write first.
- **Adult-role content** — work performance pressure, parenting, marriage, the pressure to look "blessed" or put-together in front of a faith community. This stays in the 21+ version only. It has no honest analogue in a school-age or teen's daily life, and importing it would make the lesson feel like it's talking past the room instead of to it.
