# Lesson Core Model: "Rescue or Faith?"

*Shared scaffolding — never shown to a mentor or learner directly. Built 2026-10-11 from Ian's Sunday School brainstorm and the three-board visual set (The Wholeness Test, The Yes/No Flow, Played Out). Every band file is checked against this, not against each other. Nothing in the visual set is dropped: the full logic chart is preserved verbatim below.*

## Central claim

No one is anyone else's salvation, because God — divine Love — is the only power; so the help we give is only as wise as the thought we give it from: God as the only power, the other person as able and whole, and ourselves on solid ground, able to hear wisdom and discernment.

## The picture being replaced

- **The savior thought:** whether they make it depends on me. I am carrying them; if I let go, they fall.
- **Its mirror images — the same mistake wearing different clothes:** *waiting to be rescued* (depending: my wellbeing rests on someone else being my savior), and *"I'm just trusting God"* used as a polite way of stepping back. Neither is faith. Faith stays.
- **Underneath all of it:** the belief that there is more than one power — that the problem, or the person, or I hold power apart from God.

## The picture being offered

God is the only power. The person in front of me is able and whole — capable of their own good, not needing me to survive or to make it. And I am able, too: on solid ground, calm and clear enough to hear wisdom and discernment before I act. From there, help is a gift — no strings, no scorekeeping, no panic. Salvation is not something one person does to another; it is Life, Truth, and Love understood and demonstrated, and "God worketh with you."

## The paradox, named on purpose

They may truly, humanly need your help — a ride, a meal, a hospital, a listening ear. That is real, and nothing here asks anyone to pretend otherwise or to walk away. **That was never the question. The question is the thought the help comes from.** The help can look identical; the thought behind it never does. Never resolve this paradox neatly in a lesson, and never use "wholeness" as a reason to withhold needed care.

## The bold claim, and how to hold it

If I am not on solid ground — in panic, having crowned myself the savior — I may not make wise decisions. This is supported by S&H 447:6-9, 85:4-6, 95:6-10, and 495:16-18 (below).

- **Framing rule:** always an invitation to pause, never a verdict on a past decision, never a way to shame someone who already helped imperfectly.
- **Citation honesty:** S&H 447:6-9 sits in the context of mental practice (healing). Applying it to everyday helping is Ian's extension — say so plainly if asked. Likewise 495:16-18 is written about being tempted by the illusion of sickness or sin; use it for "calm trust over fear," not as a proof-text for relationships.
- **Pause never delays a real emergency.** The flow asks about danger first.

## Learning objective family

- **Notice** — the savior thought as it shows up in real helping (panic, resentment, scorekeeping, doing it for them) and its mirror images (waiting to be rescued; "trusting God" as a way to step back).
- **Feel** — the difference between help that carries a person and help that stands beside them, felt from both sides.
- **Carry** — one repeatable practice: check the thought first (three grounds, pressure tests, the flow), and pause when "grounded" isn't there yet.
- **Locate** — one real, current situation: someone I've been "carrying," or a place where I've been "depending."

## The three grounds (Board 1, verbatim)

Step label: "STEP 1 · THREE GROUNDS TO CHECK."

- **THE ONLY POWER** — "Is God the only power here?" — "Not the problem, not them, not me. Only God has power in this moment."
- **THEM** — "Are they able and whole?" — "Capable of their own good. They don't need me to survive, to make it."
- **ME** — "Am I on solid ground?" — "Able to hear wisdom and discernment — calm, clear, unhurried — before I act."

Board 1 title: "Before you help, check the thought you're helping from." Subtitle: "The help can look identical. The thought behind it never does."

## The four pressure tests (Board 1, verbatim)

Header: "Four pressure tests." Sub: "Answer fast and honestly. A panic answer means: pause first, and come back."

1. **THE ONLY-POWER TEST** — "If this goes badly, do I feel it all rests on me?" — "Panic crowns me the savior. God is the only power."
2. **THE ABLE TEST** — "Can I name one real strength in them that this can't touch?" — "Ability is already theirs. I'm looking for it."
3. **THE QUIET TEST** — "Is the leading I hear calm and clear — or loud and rushed?" — "Wisdom is unhurried. Pressure is loud."
4. **THE THANKS TEST** — "If they never thank me or take my advice, am I still steady?" — "Needing the result means I need them to be okay."

Closing card: **THE PARADOX** — "They may truly, humanly need your help. That was never the question — the question is the thought it comes from."

## The yes/no flow — MASTER RECORD (Board 2, verbatim; do not paraphrase when building 11+ bands or the handout)

Title: "The yes/no flow." Subtitle: "Five questions. Quality of thought comes first."

- **START:** "Someone I love is struggling — and I feel the pull to step in."
- **Question 1 · SAFETY:** "Is anyone in danger right now?"
  - **YES →** ACT NOW: "Call, drive, get help — now. Their need is real, so act, and stay as calm as you can."
  - **NO →** go to Question 2.
- **Question 2 · MY GROUND:** "Am I grounded: God the only power, me able to listen?"
  - **NO →** PAUSE · GET GROUNDED: "Breathe. Pray. Listen. A hurried decision isn't wise yet. Come back when calm." (Return to Question 2.)
  - **YES →** go to Question 3.
- **Question 3 · THEIR ABILITY:** "Do I see them as able — not needing me to survive?"
  - **NO →** LOOK AGAIN: "God is their power, not me. Name one real strength in them. Then ask again." (Return to Question 3.)
  - **YES →** go to Question 4.
- **Question 4 · THEIR CHOICE:** "Did they ask for my help?" **(YES = they asked; NO = I decided.)**
  - **NO →** OFFER, DON'T IMPOSE: "'I'm here. What would help?' Then listen, and let them decide."
  - **YES →** go to Question 5.
- **Question 5 · THEIR WORK:** "Would I be doing what they can do themselves?"
  - **YES →** WALK BESIDE: "Ask questions. Name their strengths. Stay close without taking over."
  - **NO →** GIVE FREELY: "They may truly need a ride, a meal, an ear. Give it — no strings, no scorekeeping."
- **EITHER WAY (both Question 5 outcomes arrive here):** "Stay close. God is the only power, already at work in them — I'm a friend, not the source."

**Resolved by Ian (2026-10-12):** Question 4 was an either/or ("Did they ask, or did I decide?") answered yes/no. It now reads "Did they ask for my help?" in the master record and the verbatim charts. The Design-canvas graphic still carries the old wording and needs the same one-line change.

**Age adaptations of the flow (the logic is identical; the wording shifts):**
- Under 18 (11-14, 14-21 minors): ACT NOW becomes "Tell a trusted adult right away. Their need is real — you don't have to hold it alone."
- 8-11: full five-question flow in kid language (see 8-11 band and handout).
- 5-8: one rule only — "If someone is hurt, get a grown-up right now. Otherwise, ask first."

## Played Out (Board 3, verbatim)

Title: "Same moment. Two thoughts behind it." Subtitle: "The help can look identical. The thought behind it never does." Columns: "THOUGHT FROM LACK · RESCUE" / "THOUGHT FROM WHOLENESS · FAITH."

- **SPOUSE — a rough week.** Lack: "'It's all on me to fix this.' So I fix fast, hand over a plan, and quietly keep score." Wholeness: "'God is at work in them.' So I ask: help, or a listener? And do exactly that."
- **CHILD — forgot the project.** Lack: "'If I don't step in, they'll fail.' So I drive it over and panic until it's safe." Wholeness: "'They're able.' So I ask, 'What's your plan?' Stay close, and help where truly needed."
- **SIBLING — needs money again.** Lack: "'I'm their only rescue.' So I wire it and resent it, or refuse and call it tough love." Wholeness: "'I'm not their source.' So I decide what I can give freely, say it kindly, and trust them."
- **FRIEND — venting again.** Lack: "'I have to fix this pain.' So I hand out advice they never asked for." Wholeness: "'They already hold their next step.' So I listen, and they often find it."
- **PEER — a teammate is behind.** Lack: "'Nobody else will catch it.' So I quietly redo their part — and feel needed." Wholeness: "'They're capable.' So I ask what's blocking them, and give what I can freely."

Closing strip: "The paradox is real: they may truly need your hands — a ride, a meal, a hospital. Give them. Two other disguises: waiting to be rescued, and calling it 'trusting God' when it's really stepping away. Faith stays. And young children need more doing: wholeness never means leaving anyone to cope alone."

## Bridge metaphor, by age band

- **5-8:** a block tower that wobbles — helping hands that take over vs. helping hands that ask first. A hurt friend = get a grown-up.
- **8-11:** a stuck friend in a co-op game, or on a hard level or project — Controller hand vs. Coach hand; you are not your friend's battery, the power is already in them.
- **11-14:** the group chat and the feed — a friend venting again, a sibling's forgotten project, a teammate behind; the sixty-second scroll as the felt "it's all on me."
- **14-21:** a roommate, a friend's 11 p.m. text, a sibling at home after you've left, a work team, a partner — being depended on and depending, as identity and independence form.
- **21-plus:** spouse, child, adult sibling, aging parent, friend, coworker — including the caregiver who is exhausted, and the person who is the one depending.

## What does NOT inherit across bands

- **The five-question flow in verbatim wording and the "bold claim" about wise decisions:** 11-14 and up only (11-14 in softened form: "hurried, panicked help isn't wise help yet"). Under 11 gets kid-language versions with no theology stated as a proposition.
- **Marriage, parenting, caregiving, workplace, and civic framing:** 21-plus only (parenting and workplace may surface lightly at 14-21 only as the young person's own experience of being parented or on a team).
- **Addiction, mental-health, and crisis language:** never named below 21; in 21-plus keep it general ("a long, hard stretch"). For any friend-in-crisis material under 18, the action is always a trusted adult.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion questions:** never below 11.
- **A spoken-aloud moral or wrap-up line:** never for 5-8, rarely for 8-11.
- **The mirror images (waiting to be rescued; "trusting God" as stepping back):** 11-14 gently, 14-21 and 21-plus fully; under 11 not named.

## Faith-language translation (for mixed rooms)

"God is the only power" → "something good and bigger than any problem is in charge here — not the problem, not them, and not me." "Soul-sense yields to divine Mind" → "calm, clear listening before acting." "Salvation" → "being made whole, free." Always offered, never required; every faith background and none is welcome.
