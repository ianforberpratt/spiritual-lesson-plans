## What you should know
This is the age where group chats, shifting lunch tables, and who-liked-what start to feel like the realest measure of belonging in the room. Abstract thinking is coming online, so this age can genuinely grasp the distinction between the feeling of loneliness and the fact of their belonging — but self-worth is also unusually externally anchored right now, which means the honest parts of this lesson can feel exposing. Don't be surprised if the room goes quiet during the vulnerable parts; quiet is often engagement at this age, not disinterest. Give the private-journaling option real weight — it isn't a filler step, it's often the only way some kids in the room will be honest at all.

## What we're assuming
- Most kids this age have felt this ache somewhere in their actual daily life — a group chat, a lunch table, a team, a family gathering.
- Some kids may be dealing with real, ongoing social exclusion rather than an occasional off day. Stay alert to the difference.
- Phone and social-media references should stay light and familiar (texting, group chats, checking replies) rather than adult-level "doomscrolling" language — keep it close to what this age actually does with a phone.
- Nobody should be required to share out loud. Offer it; let quiet or "I'd rather not say" be a completely fine answer.

## Learning goals
1. Notice, specifically, when and where this ache shows up in their own life right now — a group chat, a lunch table, a team.
2. Feel — not just understand as a phrase — the difference between the feeling of being alone and the fact of being loved and known, even when the feeling is loud.
3. Carry one concrete alternative to reaching for a phone when the ache shows up.
4. Have at least one private, written reflection they don't have to share with anyone, plus one real person named to reach out to this week.

## How to hold the room
- Don't rush to reassurance. Naming the feeling honestly is the first part of this lesson working, not a delay before the "real" point.
- Offer the journaling step as genuinely optional to share — some kids will want to talk about it, most won't, and both are fine.
- Some kids will connect this to something heavier than an off day — being actively excluded from a group chat or a specific friend group. Let that surface; don't redirect away from it, and follow up privately after.

## The lesson flow
### Beat 1 (~7 min) — The Ache Nobody Admits
We're promised connection everywhere now — group chats, likes, being added to every event. And yet that ache still shows up, sometimes especially in a crowded lunchroom or a chat with fifty unread messages.
Discuss (pairs): "When do you feel that ache the most?" No wrong answers — this is noticing, not confessing.

### Beat 2 (~8 min) — Reaching for the Wrong Thing First
Share out loud: when the ache shows up, the instinct is to grab a phone and fill the silence — check who replied, refresh a feed. It rarely actually fills it.
Discuss (pairs): "Has checking your phone, texting, or seeing who liked something ever made the lonely feeling worse instead of better? What did you notice?"

### Beat 3 (~8 min) — Private reflection, before anything is shared
Give everyone paper. This part is not shared aloud unless someone chooses to.
Prompt: *"Where do you feel this ache the most right now? What do you usually reach for when it shows up — and does it actually help?"*
Give real silence for this — 4–5 minutes of actual quiet.

### Beat 4 (~10 min) — What's Still True Underneath
Read together: "For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord." — Romans 8:38–39
"Divine Love always has met and always will meet every human need." — Mary Baker Eddy, *Science and Health*, p. 494
Discuss (pairs or small groups of 3): "What's one thing that changes if you believe you're never actually outside of that — even in your loneliest moment, even in a group chat that's gone quiet?"

### Beat 5 (~7 min) — Reach Up, Then Reach Out
Each person names, out loud or on paper, one person they could reach out to this week.
Close with this: loneliness is a claim of absence. Love's presence is infinite. You are known, seen, and cherished — right now.

## What you'll need
- Paper and pens for each participant
- A whiteboard or shared doc, if meeting virtually

## If it's not going well
This age carries real developmental risk around social exclusion, self-worth, and comparison — this lesson can surface genuine distress, not just useful discomfort. If a private reflection, body language, or comments suggest something beyond an occasional ache — ongoing exclusion from a group, persistent hopelessness, or anything else that concerns you — don't try to resolve it in class. Follow up one-on-one, and consult the site's mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page).
