title: Two Shapes, One Loop
framing: Anticipatory grief and sudden grief are different shapes of the same real loss — this shows both, plus an honest strengths inventory for the work of adjusting.
quote: Divine Love always has met and always will meet every human need.
citation: Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 494:10
body: Two small paired diagrams side by side — "Saw It Coming" (a gradually narrowing path showing time to brace, noted "its own toll: living in the loss before it's final") and "Blindsided" (a jagged, sudden break with no runway, noted "no warning, higher chance of a harder road — not a character flaw"). Below both, a three-line strengths inventory: "A person who's real and present right now: ___" / "A practice or capacity that's real right now: ___" / "One honest thing that isn't enough some days: ___" — all three lines framed as equally true at once, none of them proof the adjusting isn't happening.
pull_line: Neither shape of grief has to earn its comfort, or finish on a schedule.
reflection_prompts:
  - Which shape (or both) fits the loss you're carrying?
  - What's one thing on your strengths list you could actually lean on this week?
