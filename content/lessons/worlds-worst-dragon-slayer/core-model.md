# Lesson Core Model: "The World's Worst Dragon Slayer"

*Source: Ian's draft, built on Science and Health p. 567:10–12. Every Science and Health quotation below was checked against the text in this Project, and the Bible quotations were checked against the KJV. This is shared scaffolding, never taught directly to any age group. Every band below is checked against this document, not against each other.*

## Why all five bands, and where the source material went

Fear wears a costume at every age, so the topic fits every band. What changes completely is the costume and the cognitive door into it. Nothing here is a downward translation.

- **5-8** gets pure experience: a hand-shadow dragon on the wall, then the room light comes on. "The dragon cannot war with them" is something a six-year-old can watch happen, because a shadow can't fight a light.
- **8-11** gets humor and a drawing that turns over: a club for terrible dragon-fighters, a dragon whose seven heads are "what if" voices, and a flip to the small, real worry on the back.
- **11-14** gets fine print: the dragon's Terms & Conditions, which everyone has clicked "I agree" on without reading, plus a rewrite exercise (technically correct versus true and kind).
- **14-21** gets a skill to practice on each other: the Dragon Interview, where the only tool is a question, plus fight / flee / refuse.
- **21-plus** gets the full version at adult stakes, with the honest tensions left open instead of resolved.

## The spiritual core (constant across all bands)

**Central claim:** The dragon is fear, lies, and hate dressed in a costume of size and noise. It has no power of its own, and there are two things it cannot make war against: Truth and Love. So the answer to a dragon is not a better weapon. It is steadiness (Truth: standing there calmly and asking what is actually here until the dragon is its real size), practical kindness (Love: the next true, kind, useful thing), and a refusal of the dragon's terms of engagement. That means not treating anyone as the enemy, and not fighting on its field.

**The picture being replaced:** Two mirror-image pictures. The first says the dragon is exactly as big as it looks, so the responsible move is to arm up: be louder, more informed, more outraged, more right. The second is its quiet twin. It says the dragon is "not real," so the spiritual move is to look away and feel nothing. The first makes a person the dragon's soldier. The second leaves the hungry person unfed and the hard conversation unhad.

**The picture being offered:** Neither fight nor flee. Truth and Love (two of the seven synonyms for God) are not weapons the dragon loses to. They are the one ground it cannot stand on. A person who stands steady, asks what specifically is at stake, and does the next kind, true thing is not winning a war. They have declined to be in one.

**Anchor citations (used at whatever depth fits the band):**
- "Truth and Love prevail against the dragon because the dragon cannot war with them." — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 567:10–12
- "The great red dragon symbolizes a lie... This dragon stands for the sum total of human error." — Science and Health, p. 563:8–10
- "But why should we stand aghast at nothingness?" — Science and Health, p. 563:7 (a lens for the person holding it, never a correction to hand someone else; see complications below)
- "Truth and Love come nearer in the hour of woe, when strong faith or spiritual strength wrestles and prevails through the understanding of God. The Gabriel of His presence has no contests." — Science and Health, p. 567:3–7
- "Divine Love always has met and always will meet every human need." — Science and Health, p. 494:10–11
- "Love is impartial and universal in its adaptation and bestowals." — Science and Health, p. 13:2–3 (the equity anchor: Love takes no side)
- "God is incorporeal, divine, supreme, infinite Mind, Spirit, Soul, Principle, Life, Truth, Love." — Science and Health, p. 465:9–10 (why Truth and Love are two names for God)
- Glossary: "RED DRAGON. Error; fear; inflammation; sensuality; subtlety; animal magnetism; envy; revenge." — Science and Health, p. 593:7–8. This grounds the draft's reading (fear, lies, hate). Band files use the plain words and leave out "animal magnetism," because a mixed-faith room can't be assumed to know the term.
- "...we have no might against this great company that cometh against us; neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee." — 2 Chronicles 20:12, KJV. This is the world's-worst-dragon-slayer verse. The verse opens by asking God to "judge them," and the chapter ends with the enemy armies destroying each other, so bands borrow only the honest-smallness posture, never the story.
- "There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear." — 1 John 4:18, KJV
- "Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good." — Romans 12:21, KJV
- Revelation 12:3, KJV: "...a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns." Revelation 12:4 (the tail sweeping stars, the threat to a newborn) appears only at 14-21 and 21-plus, in gentle summary or reading.

**Learning objective family** (same four verbs, shifting depth per band):
1. **Notice** the moment a fear, a lie, or someone's hostility has put on a costume much bigger than what's underneath it, and name the costume.
2. **Feel** (not just understand) a dragon shrink to its real size when it's met with a steady, curious question instead of a fight or a flight.
3. **Carry** one repeatable three-part move into a real moment: *What's the costume? → What's the small, real thing inside (what am I afraid of losing)? → What's one true, kind move?*
4. **Locate** one real, current dragon in their own life to size up this week, without ever having to name anyone in it.

## The bridge metaphor, by age band

| Age band | What the dragon looks like to them | Bridge used |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | A shadow on the wall, a loud noise, a big dog, a bad dream | Hand-shadow dragon and flashlight; then the room light comes on. "Big shadow, little hand — Love is the light!" |
| 8-11 | The "what if" voices: the test, lunch alone, something scary they heard | The Worst Dragon Slayers' Club: draw the scariest dragon (each head a "what if"), flip it over to the small real worry, then name the two tools |
| 11-14 | A group-chat pile-on, comparison, an AI headline, a scary feed, the same fight on loop with a friend or sibling | Dragon Terms & Conditions (five "clauses" everyone clicks "I agree" on), scenario cards, and Rewrite the Reply |
| 14-21 | Fear for the future (career and AI, the planet, the news), a loop with a parent, partner, or friend | Fight / Flee / Refuse, then the Dragon Interview: pairs practice the one question, "What specifically are you afraid of losing?" |
| 21-plus | AI at work, war, politics, the environment, the argument that never ends, and what dragon-posture models for the kids one mentors | Same structure at adult stakes, with the honest tensions held open |

## Three guardrails written into every band

1. **A person is never the dragon.** The dragon is the fear, the lie, and the hate wrapped around a problem. It is never a person, party, nation, faith, or side of an issue. This is externalizing: the problem is the problem, the person is not the problem. It also makes the lesson bridge-safe, because no vantage in the room is "the dragon."
2. **Refusing the dragon's terms is not staying close to someone unsafe.** "Truth and Love" never means reaching out kindly to someone who is hurting you. The loving move can be a boundary or telling a trusted adult. Cross-linked to When Someone Feels Like the Enemy at every band from 8-11 up.
3. **The lesson never adjudicates the issue.** AI, war, the climate, and politics appear only as places a fear can live. The lesson addresses the posture, never the policy, so any vantage can sit in the room.

## Two honest complications the lesson holds instead of resolving

- **It can feel like a fight.** The same page has Michael ("spiritual strength," who "fights the holy wars," 566:30–567:1) beside Gabriel ("no contests," 567:6–7), and Truth and Love that "wrestle and prevail." Both are true: the felt experience of wrestling, and the deeper fact of no contest. Younger bands never need this. 14-21 carries it as a facilitator's pocket note, and 21-plus opens it as a discussion question.
- **The dragon is a lie, not the suffering.** Science and Health 563:7 ("why should we stand aghast at nothingness?") is a lens for the person holding it. It is never a correction to hand someone in real pain. The costume can be false while the loss, the need, and the feeling underneath are completely real. Love goes straight to the need.

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **Political, geopolitical, and war material** is 21-plus only. 14-21 gets "the news, the planet, the future" as general big-world fears. 11-14 gets an AI headline and a scary feed. 8-11 gets "something scary you heard." 5-8 gets none.
- **The Dragon Interview** (peers practicing on real fears) is 14-21 and 21-plus only. It needs a listener who can hold a real fear without fixing it, and a speaker who can pick a medium-weight dragon. 8-11 gets a lighter partner-draw. 11-14 gets private-first writing and scenario cards, never peers interviewing each other's real fears.
- **Fight / Flee / Refuse** is 14-21 and 21-plus. 11-14 gets the Terms & Conditions clauses instead, so the two framings never crowd each other.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion questions** are never below 11, and 11-14 gets a private-reflection option before any sharing.
- **A spoken-aloud "moral" or wrap-up sentence** is never for 5-8 and essentially never for 8-11.
- **The words "enemy," "war," and "killed by divine Principle"** (567:9–10) stay out of 5-8 and 8-11.
- **The Michael/Gabriel tension** and "is refusing a luxury of people with small dragons?" are 21-plus discussion questions only.
