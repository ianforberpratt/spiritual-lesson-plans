# Lesson Core Model: "God Lights Every Candle"

*Source lesson: Christian Science Bible Lesson "Are Sin, Disease, and Death Real?" (Oct 5–11, 2026). The thread pulled from it: salvation belongs to God, each person works out their own with God, and God is with everyone whether or not they know it.*

## Central claim

Each person's relationship with God is their own, between them and God. We can't hand it to anyone, measure it for anyone, or be anyone's savior, and we don't need to, because God is already with every person, "though thou hast not known me." Our part is to live what we love, share it freely, and let the other person be exactly who they are.

## Language note (for the build and for facilitators)

Lead with **"relationship with God"** in every band, since it's open to every faith background in the room. Do not avoid or soften **"salvation"**: it's Mary Baker Eddy's word and the Bible Lesson's word. Introduce it through the passages, defined by her own sentence ("Life, Truth, and Love understood and demonstrated as supreme over all; sin, sickness, and death destroyed," p. 593:20), and let participants meet it there rather than making it the entry point.

## The picture being replaced

- "If I really care about you, I have to get you to see it my way."
- "If you worship differently, something must be wrong or missing, and it's my job to fix it."
- A quieter version: *I* am responsible for how your relationship with God turns out.

## The picture being offered

God lights each candle. Salvation belongs to God, and each of us works out our own with God, who "worketh with you." Our part is to let our own light shine honestly and to love people well while theirs burns in its own way. We share without needing a particular response. We exemplify without converting.

## Facilitator posture (stated plainly, not attributed to Eddy)

**"Different, not wrong" is the facilitator's posture. It is not a claim that Eddy makes.** The passages below support it through (a) God's universality, (b) "work out your own salvation according to their light," and (c) her call to charity toward "differing forms of religion." She does not say every path is equivalent, and this lesson never says so on her behalf. Facilitators should hold it as: *I can hold what I love and fully respect that your way of worshiping is yours.* If a participant asks "so does Christian Science say all religions are the same?" the honest answer is no, and it also says "judge not" and "never condemn rashly." That honest answer is the lesson, not a problem with it.

## Two guardrails built into every band

1. **Not "not my problem."** Letting go of anyone's beliefs is not the same as letting go of anyone's safety. We don't control what anyone believes. But when someone is unsafe, hurt, or in danger, we always bring in a caring adult. That is love, not conversion.
2. **A concrete test for sharing vs. converting.** *Do I need them to respond a certain way for this to count as a good conversation?* If yes, I've slid from sharing into steering. This is the repeatable "Carry" practice in every band.

## Learning objective family

- **Notice**: the pull to bring someone along, fix their way of worshiping, or carry responsibility for their relationship with God.
- **Feel**: the relief of not being anyone's savior, and the steadiness of knowing God is already with them.
- **Carry**: one repeatable test: *Do I need them to respond a certain way for this to count?*
- **Locate**: one real, current relationship where this applies.

## Bridge metaphor by age band

- **8–11**: A friend whose family does different things on weekends or before meals. The pull to say "my way is the right way," or the happy surprise of being curious instead.
- **11–14**: A friend asks why you believe what you believe, or you feel the pull to bring a friend along to everything. Group chats and comparison ("my family's is the real one").
- **14–21**: A roommate, friend, or partner with a different faith or none; classmates; leaving the faith you grew up in or staying in it, with people who see it differently. The urge to fix, defend, or avoid.
- **21+**: A spouse or partner, in-laws, adult children, coworkers, neighbors, the family table, parenting, civic and political life. The cost of needing agreement.

## What does NOT inherit across bands

- **5–8 is deliberately not built.** "Different families pray differently, and that's okay" works as a story, but there is little to *do* with the deeper idea at that age. Flagged as a gap, not an oversight.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion** (e.g., "What does it mean to be responsible for another's relationship with God?") stays 11+. At 8–11 the same idea is carried by concrete scenarios and an activity.
- **Spoken-aloud wrap-up moral**: never in 8–11. Let the activity carry it.
- **Family or community conflict over leaving, joining, or changing a faith**: 14–21 in generalized form ("people you love who see this differently"), and fuller in 21+.
- **Marriage, parenting, workplace, and civic/political examples**: 21+ only.
- **Anything implying a participant's own beliefs or family are "wrong"**: out of every band.

## Handout decision

**Wallet card, same across bands.** The whole takeaway compresses into one quote (Psalms 18:28), a short reframe, one memorable line, and three short questions. The bridge metaphors differ by band, but the quote and the three questions make sense to every band without changing.
