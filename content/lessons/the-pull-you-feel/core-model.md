# Lesson Core Model: "The Pull You Feel"

*A lesson on romantic attraction — how to tell the difference between lust, human love, and
divine Love, and how to let the last one govern the other two. Sensitive material; built starting
at 11-14 only, with the 11-14 delivery reframed away from romantic/partner language per the
site's sexual-and-romantic-specificity rule (section 5). See "What does NOT get inherited" below
for exactly what stays out of 11-14.*

## Why not 5-8 or 8-11

Attraction to another person as a romantic pull — wanting someone, feeling pulled toward them,
navigating the difference between a healthy and an unhealthy version of that pull — isn't a lived
experience for concrete-operational kids in any form this lesson could honestly deliver. There's
no reframe that makes this topic true to a 6- or 9-year-old's actual daily life the way a crush
is true to an 11-year-old's. Default to not building it.

**Bands built:** 11-14, 14-21, 21-plus only.

## The spiritual core (constant across all built bands)

**Central claim:** The pull you feel toward another person isn't automatically good or automatically
dangerous — it's information you can test. What the source texts below call lust — this lesson's
own voice calls it the out-of-tune pull — is love's material counterfeit: a pull that runs on
urgency, secrecy, and getting something for yourself. It's the feeling of being tempted and not
aligned with who you are, not comfortable in your own thoughts. Human love is real, but on its own
it can still be anxious, possessive, or self-focused. Divine Love — God, the one Principle governing
everyone — is the actual source; when human affection lets divine Love govern it, it grows instead
of grabs. It brings harmony and peace to both people, not just one.

**The picture being replaced:** that attraction is either (a) just chemistry, follow it wherever
it pulls, or (b) a temptation to be gritted through and mistrusted outright. Both pictures put
matter — the body, the feeling, the other person — in charge. Neither one teaches a person to
actually *look at* the feeling and ask what it's made of.

**The picture being offered:** attraction can be tested, gently and without shame, the same way
you'd check which way a compass needle is actually pointing. It isn't your enemy and it isn't your
master. Brought to Love (God) and asked "what do you want to become — harmony, or hunger?" — it
matures instead of ruling. This works exactly the same regardless of who the person is or what
shape the relationship takes: the test is never about *who* you're drawn to, only about *what kind
of pull it is.*

**A note on language:** the citations below use the word "lust" — that's the source texts' own
vocabulary, quoted exactly and never altered. But in the lesson's own voice — the surrounding
prose, the dial, the discussion prompts, every place a facilitator or learner is doing the
talking — that word is replaced with "the out-of-tune pull," "tempted and not aligned with who you
are," or "not comfortable in your own thoughts." The goal is to name the feeling clearly without it
ever landing as a label or a verdict on the person having it. Ian asked for this explicitly: "lust"
should describe something you don't want as part of your experience, not something you are.

## Learning objective family

1. **Notice** which kind of pull is actually happening the moment attraction shows up — the
   anxious, secrecy-needing kind, or the settled, honest kind — instead of only sorting it out
   months later.
2. **Feel**, not just agree with intellectually, that the intensity of a feeling isn't the same
   thing as its truth. A pull can be strong and still not be the truth about a person's worth, the
   other person's worth, or what's actually good for either of them.
3. **Carry** a repeatable test — "the litmus test" / the dial — into a real moment of attraction,
   rather than a rule to follow blindly or a feeling to suppress blindly.
4. **Locate** one real, current person or situation to (privately, or with a trusted person) run
   the test on.

## The bridge, by age band

| Age band | What this looks like in real daily life right now | Bridge used |
|---|---|---|
| 11-14 | A crush — wanting to know everything about someone, feeling jittery, wanting them to notice you, maybe feeling weirdly jealous or wanting to change yourself to get their attention | "The grabby feeling" vs. "the warm feeling" — plain, concrete, no romantic-partner or dating language, no word "lust." Litmus question: "Does liking this person make me more me, or less me?" |
| 14-21 | First real relationships, the "talking stage," texting intensity, pressure — from a partner or from culture — to move fast physically or emotionally, forming identity partly through a relationship | The full out-of-tune-pull / love / Love triad, named directly (with "lust" appearing only inside direct citations). The dial tool used explicitly. Explicit statement that the test is identical regardless of orientation or relationship shape |
| 21-plus | Marriage, established partnerships, an inconvenient or uninvited pull toward someone who isn't a spouse or partner, dating again after divorce or loss, discerning a "close friendship" that's drifting somewhere | Full theological depth — animal magnetism named and explained, the harmony test broadened to everyone touched by the choice (not just the two people in the moment), temperance as ongoing practice rather than one-time willpower |

## The tool: the litmus test / the dial

A pull toward another person can be held up to the light and tested, the same way you'd check
which way a compass needle actually points — or a dial actually needs tuning. Picture a dial with
two ends. One end is the material counterfeit — called "the out-of-tune pull" at 14-21 and
21-plus, "the grabby feeling" at 11-14; the word "lust" itself appears only inside the direct
citations, never as the lesson's own label for the feeling. The other end is the real thing (Love,
the source, expressed as patient, respectful human love).

Paired signs, used at full depth for 14-21/21-plus and in reframed kid-language for 11-14:

| Out-of-tune / grabby end | In-tune end (Love, expressed as love) |
|---|---|
| Needs secrecy to survive | Can stand in daylight |
| Urgency — "now, or I'll lose it" | Patience — can wait, isn't afraid of time |
| Wants to possess or control | Respects the other person's freedom |
| Focused on getting something | Wants to give and bless, not just receive |
| Narrows — the world shrinks to this one craving | Expands — you're warmer toward everyone, not just this one person |
| Leaves anxiety, disquiet, self-doubt | Leaves harmony, calm, more sense of self, not less |
| Sees a feeling to chase | Sees a whole person, individual and unchanged, still fully themselves |

This table is the seed for the lesson's required downloadable resource — see the handout file.
The 11-14 version and the 14-21/21-plus version use different vocabulary for the two ends of the
dial (see "What does NOT get inherited" and the handout file for why they aren't shared as-is).

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **The word "lust," and any direct quotation of it,** stays out of the 11-14 delivery entirely.
  The underlying concept (a grabby, self-serving pull vs. a warm, expansive one) carries down; the
  vocabulary and the II Peter and p. 584:17 citations do not. This mirrors how the site already
  handles this category (see `talking-about-pornography-use`, `not-the-struggle`).
- **The word "lust" as a label,** full stop, stays out of every band's own voice — including
  14-21 and 21-plus. It appears only inside direct quotations of scripture or Science and Health,
  quoted exactly. Everywhere the lesson is speaking for itself — section titles, discussion prompts,
  the dial — it uses "the out-of-tune pull" (or "tempted and not aligned with who you are," "not
  comfortable in your own thoughts") instead, so the word names a feeling and never becomes a
  verdict on the person having it.
- **The DEVIL definition (p. 584:17), with its "animal magnetism or hypnotism" language,** stays
  out of 11-14 and is introduced only at 14-21 (used at moderate depth) and 21-plus (used at full
  depth, named and explained explicitly as this site's language for a cultural/personal spell of
  self-deception).
- **Dating, "partner," and romantic-relationship language** stays out of 11-14. That band uses
  "someone I like" / crush language only — no assumption anyone is or should be dating.
- **Explicit discussion of physical/sexual pressure or pacing** stays out of 11-14 and 14-21 is
  handled only in general terms ("pressure to move faster than feels honest"), never graphically.
  It's addressed most directly at 21-plus, still without graphic content, per the site's
  "warm about hard things, never clinical" voice.
- **The S&H 588:9–19 "individuality" passage** is the one exception that travels safely all the
  way down — it's about identity and belonging to one Mind, not romance, and its "numbers which
  never blend" image is concrete enough for 11-14 to hold directly. It is quoted at 11-14 (in
  full, with a plain-language explanation attached) as well as at the older bands.
- **Full open discussion** stays out of 11-14 in favor of private reflection, per the site's
  standard pattern for this age (see `not-the-struggle` 11-14) — nobody names their crush out loud
  unless they choose to.

## A note the facilitator script should carry at every band

This lesson does not assume, and should never imply, a particular gender, orientation, or
relationship structure. The litmus test is the same test regardless of who a person is drawn to —
it tests the *pull*, never the *person* it's aimed at. Say this plainly in the room; don't leave it
implied.
