## What you should know
Nothing required. Christian Science refers to this as an "angel message." Other traditions name it a still small voice, conscience, intuition, or gut feeling. This lesson doesn't require shared vocabulary — just recognition that the experience itself is familiar.

## What we're assuming

- Everyone has felt some inner "wait," "not yet," or "go another way," whether framed spiritually or not.

## Learning goals
1. Understands inner nudges appear across traditions under different names, and that noticing them is learnable, not an exclusive gift.
2. Possesses a concrete framework: guidance feels calm, quiet, certain; pressure feels rushed, urgent, demanding control.
3. Has named one real moment — significant or small — where they felt a nudge and their response.
4. Recognizes that ignoring nudges is acceptable; the practice itself, not perfect listening, matters.

## How to hold the room
- Some will use faith language; others prefer "intuition" or "gut feeling." Don't correct their terminology.
- Avoid framing as "if you'd listened better, harm wouldn't have occurred." This isn't about assigning blame for difficult experiences; it's about building practice.

## The lesson flow
### Beat 1 (~10 min) — Think back 48 hours
Was there a moment you felt a sudden nudge — a quiet "wait," a "not now," a "go left instead of right"? Did you follow it, or push through anyway? No wrong answer either way — just notice.

### Beat 2 (~10 min) — What do you call this?
Across many traditions exists a name for this inner prompting. Discuss: What's your word for it, if you have one?

### Beat 3 (~15 min) — Telling the real thing from the noise
"Real guidance tends to feel calm, quiet, and certain — it doesn't need to control the outcome, and it usually invites you toward stillness. Anxious pressure tends to feel rushed, forceful, urgent — it needs to be right, and it needs control right now."

Try it: Think of a recent decision made under pressure. Which one was actually steering — the quiet version or the loud version?

### Beat 4 (~10 min) — Practicing on the small stuff
The reason to practice on low-stakes moments is that hearing the real voice clearly becomes harder when stakes are high. Close with this: you don't have to get this right every time. Noticing the difference between quiet and loud voices itself constitutes the practice.

## What you'll need
- Paper and pens — or a shared doc/whiteboard for virtual meetings
- Optional printables: Visual ["Static, or Signal?"](/assets/materials/learning-to-hear-the-nudge/static-or-signal.html); Pocket card ["The Nudge Log"](/assets/materials/learning-to-hear-the-nudge/nudge-log.html)

## If it's not going well
If someone discloses an inner voice directing self-harm or harm to others, that isn't this lesson's "nudge" — stop and address directly with that person. Never suggest something bad happening resulted from insufficient listening. Follow the site's mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page) for next steps.
