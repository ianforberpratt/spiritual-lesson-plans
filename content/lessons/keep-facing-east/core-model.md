# Lesson Core Model: "Keep Facing East"

*Source: Ian's reflection "One way to heaven? Sheesh, that's a lot of pressure," written from the
Christian Science Quarterly Bible Lesson on "Reality." This is shared scaffolding, never taught
directly to any age group. Every band below is checked against this document, not against each
other.*

## Why five bands, and what carried where

The fear underneath this lesson — *I'm doing it wrong, and there's one narrow way I keep falling
off of* — shows up at every age, just wearing different clothes. A five-year-old feels it as
"I'm always getting it wrong." A twelve-year-old feels it as "everyone else got the instructions
but me." A nineteen-year-old feels it as "if I question the faith I grew up in, am I off the
road?" An adult feels it in prayer, parenting, and a long tired stretch that seems like proof of
failure. That's why all five bands fit naturally.

What does *not* carry down is the phrase "one way to heaven" itself. Heaven-as-destination and
"one way" theology are abstract, and for a young child they easily become a new fear rather than
a relief. So 5-8 and 8-11 get the sunrise and the turning, never the doctrine. The theological
unpacking of "one way" begins at 11-14 in simple form and becomes a real text discussion at 14-21
and 21-plus.

Ian's personal situation in the source reflection (a current healing, fatigue, angry thoughts) is
**not** carried into any band as content. The 21-plus band invites the facilitator to share their
own honest current stretch *if they choose*, in the same spirit — never Ian's specifics.

## The spiritual core (constant across all built bands)

**Central claim:** The "one way" is not a narrow route, a checklist, or a denomination you can
fall off of — it is a consciousness and a posture: seeing that good, God, is the only reality,
yielding to it, and following where truth leads. That way is open from wherever a person is
standing, and the light it faces keeps rising for everyone.

**The picture being replaced:** a single thin road to God, with me somewhere off to the side of
it, squinting — always a little afraid I'm doing prayer, faith, or life "wrong," and treating a
hard or tired stretch, or my own self-criticism, as proof that I've failed.

**The picture being offered:** a sunrise. The sun comes up every morning, looks a little
different depending on where you're standing, and doesn't skip anybody. You don't have to force
the sunrise. You keep facing east. The "narrow" part isn't the path — it's where I'm looking, and
what I'm honestly after. That's *more* accountability, not less: no one grades my homework except
me and Truth, and Truth doesn't grade on a curve. But self-condemnation isn't the narrow way —
honest, continued seeking is.

**A note on the father's cry (facilitator background only):** Eddy also quotes Mark 9:24 on p.
23:27–28, in a passage contrasting "blind faith" with faith "advanced to spiritual understanding"
(p. 23:17–19). The lessons honor the father's honesty as a real beginning, and the 21-plus band
gently adds that faith keeps growing from there, using the p. 23:17–19 line. The "blind faith"
comment itself is not raised as a discussion point in any band. If a participant brings it up,
welcome it honestly: the cry is a doorway, not a destination.

**A reading note on "cycles of divine light":** in context (p. 135), Eddy is describing how the
demonstration of Truth recurs across history. The lesson's sunrise image (light keeps coming
around, for everyone) is a faithful devotional reading of that phrase, not a claim about its
technical meaning. Facilitators at 21-plus can say so if asked.

**Learning objective family** (same four verbs, shifting depth per band):
1. **Notice** the "am I doing it wrong?" squint when it shows up — in prayer, school, friendships,
   work, family.
2. **Feel** the difference between walking a tightrope you can fall off and turning toward a
   light that keeps rising — not just understand it as an idea.
3. **Carry** one repeatable question into a real moment: *Not "Am I on the right road?" — "Which
   way am I facing?"* (older bands add: *"What am I honestly after?"*)
4. **Locate** one real, current place where they've been squinting, and try the question there.

## The bridge metaphor, by age band

| Age band | What "doing it wrong" looks like to them | Bridge metaphor used |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | Getting corrected, coloring outside the lines, not being as big or fast as others | A small, crooked sunflower who worries she's growing wrong — and just turns to face the sun |
| 8-11 | A game where one mistake sends you back to the start; a test; trying to be "good" | Tightrope vs. compass — a masking-tape line you can fall off, and a paper sun everyone can point to from anywhere |
| 11-14 | Everyone else seems to have the instructions; grades, friend groups, "am I praying right?" | The squint (off the side of a narrow road) vs. the sunrise; the father's honest one-sentence prayer |
| 14-21 | Questioning the faith you grew up in; leaving home; friends of other faiths; "am I off the road?" | "One way" as a road vs. "one way" as a consciousness — read side by side with Matthew 7 and John 14 |
| 21-plus | Prayer, parenting, marriage, career, a long tired stretch; "one right road" thinking about other people too | The same road-vs-sunrise question at adult stakes, plus the two ditches: complacency and self-condemnation |

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **"One way to heaven" doctrine and the word "heaven" as a destination** — not used at 5-8 or
  8-11. Young children receive the sunrise and the turning only. Introduced in simple form at
  11-14; real text discussion at 14-21 and 21-plus.
- **Any critique of other traditions' "one way" beliefs** — never, in any band. Many sincere
  people hold a "one way" belief out of real love for God and a real wish not to get it wrong.
  Every band that touches this says so plainly. The lesson offers a different picture; it does
  not argue anyone out of theirs.
- **Self-condemnation, guilt, and "am I failing?" language** — named directly at 14-21 and
  21-plus (S&H 455:3). At 11-14 it appears gently as "being hard on yourself." Not named at all
  below 11; the sunflower and compass carry it.
- **Illness, fatigue, and healing language** — 21-plus only, and only as a facilitator's
  optional honest share or a participant's own choice. Never Ian's specifics.
- **"Faith growing into understanding" (S&H p. 23:17–19)** — 21-plus only, as a gentle
  extension of the father's story. Younger bands keep the father's honesty as the whole point.
- **Civic "one right road" thinking about other people (politics, parenting styles, worship)** —
  21-plus only, as a short bridge-building extension, never partisan.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion questions** — never below 11. 11-14 uses private reflection
  with an optional share.
- **A spoken-aloud moral or wrap-up** — never at 5-8, essentially never at 8-11.
