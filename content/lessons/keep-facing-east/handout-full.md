title: A Road or a Sunrise?
framing: I used to picture one narrow road to God, and me somewhere off to the side of it, squinting. What if the one way was never a road at all — but which way I'm facing?
quote: "There is but one way to heaven, harmony, and Christ in divine Science shows us this way. It is to know no other reality — to have no other consciousness of life — than good, God and His reflection."
citation: Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 242:9–13
body: |
  A simple horizon line across the page with a half-risen sun on the right (east) side.

  Under it, two columns side by side, headed "A Road I Could Fall Off" and "A Sunrise I Can Face,"
  each with a small icon (a thin road with a figure standing beside it; a rising sun).

  Paired lines, one row per pair, under the two headers:
  - Road: One narrow line — step off and you're out / Sunrise: Comes up every morning, for everyone
  - Road: Someone else holds the map / Sunrise: My relationship with God is mine
  - Road: I'm off to the side, squinting / Sunrise: Wherever I'm standing, I can turn and face it
  - Road: A tired stretch means I failed / Sunrise: A tired stretch might just be early morning
  - Road: Beating myself up keeps me on track / Sunrise: Honest seeking keeps me facing the light

  A short strip across the bottom of the columns, three words with one line each:
  - SEE — what's true: good, God, is the real thing
  - YIELD — stop pushing; let it lead
  - FOLLOW — take the next honest step
pull_line: "**I don't have to force the sunrise. I just keep facing east.**"

  - "One place I've been squinting lately, sure I'm doing it wrong:"
