# Lesson Core Model: "Scared About the News"

## Central claim
Fear is a real, worth-noticing feeling — but it has never been evidence about who you are or what's ultimately true. You are the same whole, spiritual idea of God whether the news is calm or the news is loud, and that unshaken identity is what lets you actually show up for someone else instead of being drained by carrying fear alone.

## The picture being replaced
Something scary happening — near or far, big or small — means the world isn't safe, I'm not safe, and it's on me to figure out, alone, how to protect myself or the people I love.

## The picture being offered
You are already whole and held, exactly as you are, right now, no matter what's happening close by or far away. Fear is a real signal to notice and bring somewhere — not proof about your safety or your worth. That groundedness is what actually frees you to help when it's your turn.

## Learning objective family
- **Notice** — the difference between a real feeling of fear and the story that feeling tells about "the world isn't safe" or "I'm not safe."
- **Feel** — not just understand intellectually — that they are already whole and held, unchanged by what's loud in the world around them.
- **Carry** — one repeatable practice or question for the next time something scary reaches them, sized to their own life.
- **Locate** — one real, current thing that's actually scaring them right now, named honestly rather than kept vague.

## The bridge metaphor, by age band
- **5-8:** A loud thunderstorm, a scary part of a story or movie, or overhearing a grown-up sound worried on the phone. Fully concrete, fully in-the-room. No literal "news."
- **8-11:** Hearing about something scary that happened somewhere else — a flood on the TV in the next room, a fire a classmate's family went through, something a friend brings up at recess. First brush with a wider world beyond home, secondhand.
- **11-14:** Something upsetting that scrolls by on a phone, or a friend group buzzing anxiously about something online. Many kids this age are getting their own first real exposure to a feed, right as self-worth is unusually externally anchored.
- **14-21:** Sustained, direct exposure to real news and social media — AI, politics, disasters, doomscrolling — layered onto the already-near-adult task of figuring out their own place in an uncertain world.
- **21-plus:** Full adult scope. AI, division, natural disasters, civic and family responsibility, the real question of "how do I prepare" for a world you can't predict or control.

## What does NOT inherit across bands
- Literal discussion of "the news," AI, geopolitics, or named current disasters — reserved for 14-21 and 21-plus. Younger bands get concrete, story-scale, in-the-room fears, never framed as news consumption.
- Any discussion question asking kids to reflect abstractly on division or unity as a social concept — reserved for 11-14 and up. Younger bands get the same truth through story and repetition, never through the abstraction itself.
- Explicit "master your own reactive impulses" language (anger, revenge, deceit) — reserved for 11-14 and up, where self-awareness of internal reaction is developmentally available. Younger bands get a simpler version: "what do we do with a big scared feeling."
- A spoken-aloud "moral" — never for 5-8, rare for 8-11. The story and the activity carry it.
- `sensitive_topic` flag: 14-21 and 21-plus are flagged `requires-careful-adaptation`, since real disasters may surface personal grief or loss for someone in the room; younger bands are `none`.
