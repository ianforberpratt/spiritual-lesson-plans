## What you should know

Full adult scope here — AI, division, real disasters, civic and family responsibility, the honest instinct to want to prepare for something you can't predict. Someone in the room may have a personal connection to a disaster or loss discussed here — hold that possibility with real care. Nothing you don't already have for prep beyond the printable.

## What we're assuming

- Most adults in this room are already carrying some version of this fear, whether or not they'd say so out loud in a group.
- A wide range of faith backgrounds, political views, and life circumstances — no assumed shared framework beyond a willingness to look honestly at fear together.
- Someone here may be personally affected by a disaster or loss named in this lesson.

## Learning goals

- Leave able to name, specifically, what's actually driving their own fear right now.
- Understand "being prepared" as rootedness in identity rather than control over outcomes.
- Carry a specific, repeatable practice for the next time fear about the world shows up.
- Leave with one small, concrete act of care to offer someone else this week.

## How to hold the room

- This is not a room to perform having it figured out — model your own honest uncertainty as the facilitator.
- If someone shares a personal loss connected to a named disaster, let the room sit with it rather than rushing past.
- Political and cultural differences will be present in the room — hold the conversation at the level of shared fear and shared identity, not at the level of taking sides on current events.
- You're not resolving the state of the world by the end of the hour. Sitting with the tension honestly is the actual work.

## The lesson flow

### Beat 1 (~6 min) — Naming the panic honestly

Ask: "What's actually keeping you up at night about the state of things — AI, division, disasters, something closer to home? No need to solve it, just name it."

Let a few people answer. Let it be real.

### Beat 2 (~8 min) — Who you already are

Read together:

> So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them. — Genesis 1:27, NIV

Say: "That's the whole prep plan, honestly. You don't prepare for chaos by predicting it — you can't. You prepare by knowing, ahead of time, who you already are: whole, untouched at the core, not naive to what's happening but not defined by it either."

### Beat 3 (~8 min) — The division underneath the fear

Read together:

> There is neither Jew nor Gentile, neither slave nor free, nor is there male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus. — Galatians 3:28, NIV

Discuss: "The divisions we scroll past every day — what would it actually mean, practically, if they were never the truth about us? Not naive, not pretending disagreement doesn't exist — just not the deepest truth."

### Beat 4 (~9 min) — Choosing which fire to feed

Tell the story of Nabal and Abigail (1 Samuel 25) briefly: a wealthy, churlish man provokes David to the edge of violence over wounded pride; a woman named Abigail rides out unarmed and talks the whole thing back from the edge with wisdom instead of a weapon.

> A person's wisdom yields patience; it is to one's glory to overlook an offense. — Proverbs 19:11, NIV

> Christian Science commands man to master the propensities, — to hold hatred in abeyance with kindness, ... revenge with charity, and to overcome deceit with honesty. — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 405:5-9

Discuss: "Where have you noticed your own fear about the world curdling into outrage at a specific person — online, at work, at your own dinner table? What would it look like to feed a different fire?"

### Beat 5 (~10 min) — Semper paratus

> Divinity is always ready. Semper paratus is Truth's motto. — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 458:14-15

Say: "Semper paratus — it's literally the Coast Guard's motto, borrowed from a much older idea. Readiness was never about scrambling to get ahead of the next disaster. It's a description of God. If that's true, preparation stops being about stockpiling. It's staying rooted enough in wholeness that when your moment to help someone comes, you're not too drained by your own fear to actually help."

Discuss: "What's one place in your life right now where you're stockpiling fear instead of staying ready to actually help?"

### Beat 6 (~4 min) — What to carry

Close with the practice: today or this week, reach out to one person who might be carrying this same fear — a text, a call, five minutes — and tell them they're not carrying it alone either.

## If it's not going well

- **Silence:** Normal for adults sitting with something honest — let it hold, don't rush to fill it.
- **Pushback or disagreement**, including political disagreement about the state of the world: Welcome. Redirect toward the shared human experience of fear underneath the disagreement, not toward resolving the disagreement itself.
- **A strong emotional reaction, or someone shares a personal connection to a real disaster or loss:** Pause, ground the room, check in with that person privately, and don't rush back to the agenda.
- **A disclosure of real harm:** Stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, and point to the site's mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations.
