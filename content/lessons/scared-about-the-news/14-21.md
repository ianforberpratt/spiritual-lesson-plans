## What you should know

This age group is old enough for the real content — actual news, actual disasters, actual AI anxiety — anchored in the independence-and-identity stage they're actually in: figuring out what kind of adult they're going to be in a world that feels unstable. Some in the room may be personally connected to a real disaster or loss; hold that possibility gently rather than treating every example as hypothetical. Nothing you don't already have for prep beyond the printable.

## What we're assuming

- Most in this room already doomscroll, in some form, and already feel some version of the fear this lesson names.
- A range of faith backgrounds and starting points — no assumed shared belief or shared trauma.
- Someone in the room may have a real, personal connection to a disaster or loss mentioned here — don't treat every example as purely abstract.

## Learning goals

- Name, specifically, what's actually scaring them right now — AI, division, disasters, the future, or something else entirely.
- Understand readiness as a description of an already-present reality, not a task to complete.
- Carry one repeatable practice into a real moment of scrolling or hearing hard news this week.
- Leave with one small, concrete way to turn fear into an actual act of care for someone else.

## How to hold the room

- Let real vulnerability land without rushing to fix it or reframe it as a lesson learned.
- If someone brings up a real, personal loss connected to a disaster, receive it without needing the room to move on quickly.
- Offer an explicit way to pass on sharing at any point.
- You're not trying to resolve the world's uncertainty neatly by the end of the hour — sitting with it honestly matters more than closing it.

## The lesson flow

### Beat 1 (~6 min) — Naming the fear honestly

Ask: "What's actually been scaring you lately — AI, the news, something happening in the world, something closer to home? You don't have to explain it, just name the category."

Let a few answer. Don't rush to resolve anything yet — just let the room agree this is common and real.

### Beat 2 (~9 min) — Fear, named and held

Read together:

> Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have summoned you by name; you are mine. Since you are precious and honored in my sight, and because I love you... — Isaiah 43:1, 4, NIV

Discuss: "Notice what this doesn't say. It doesn't say the scary thing isn't real, or that you shouldn't feel it. What does it say instead?"

### Beat 3 (~9 min) — The division underneath the fear

Say: "A lot of the scariest parts of the world right now aren't just disasters — they're how divided everyone seems. It can feel like the ground itself is splitting."

Read together:

> There is neither Jew nor Gentile, neither slave nor free, nor is there male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus. — Galatians 3:28, NIV

Discuss: "This was written into a world at least as divided as ours. What do you think it's claiming was actually true the whole time, underneath the division?"

### Beat 4 (~9 min) — Choosing which fire to feed

Read together:

> Christian Science commands man to master the propensities, — to hold hatred in abeyance with kindness, ... revenge with charity, and to overcome deceit with honesty. — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 405:5-9

Discuss: "Where have you noticed your own scared feelings turning into anger at someone — online or in person — who probably didn't deserve the full weight of it? What would it look like to feed a different fire?"

### Beat 5 (~9 min) — Semper paratus

Say: "There's a phrase buried in Science and Health that doesn't get quoted much."

Read together:

> Divinity is always ready. Semper paratus is Truth's motto. — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 458:14-15

Say: "Semper paratus — always ready. It's literally the Coast Guard's motto, borrowed from a much older idea. Readiness was never about you scrambling to get ahead of the next disaster. It's a description of God. If that's true, being prepared stops being about stockpiling — it's about staying rooted enough that when your moment to help comes, you're not too drained by your own fear to actually show up."

Discuss: "What's one concrete way you could be 'ready' this week — not for a disaster, but for a person?"

### Beat 6 (~3 min) — What to carry

Close with the practice: name one person you'll reach out to this week — a text, a call, five minutes — who might be carrying some of this same fear, and let them know they're not carrying it alone either.

## If it's not going well

- **Silence:** Normal — let it sit; this age often needs a beat before speaking honestly.
- **Pushback or disagreement** (e.g., "the world really is falling apart"): Welcome, not a problem — don't argue it away; the point is narrower than whether hard things are real.
- **A strong emotional reaction, or someone shares a personal connection to a real disaster or loss:** Pause, ground the room, check in with that person privately, and don't rush the group back to the agenda.
- **A disclosure of real harm:** Stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, and point to the site's mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations.
