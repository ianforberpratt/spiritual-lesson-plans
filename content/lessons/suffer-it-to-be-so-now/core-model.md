# Lesson Core Model: "Suffer It to Be So, Now"

*Source: Ian's blog post of the same name, built around Matthew 3:15 — Jesus asking John to baptize
him at the Jordan, and John's confusion about it. This core model follows the established pattern:
one spiritual core, independently-designed age-band deliveries. This document is shared
scaffolding; it is never taught directly.*

## The spiritual core (constant across all built bands)

**Central claim:** Belovedness — permission to be exactly where you are, unfinished and all — comes
first, not last. God's love isn't the reward that arrives once the fixing is finished; it's already
true, in the middle of the unresolved thing, before any of the "work" has even started.

**The picture being replaced:** that rest, love, wholeness, or belonging has to be earned — that a
person gets to feel it once the hard part is over, the mess is cleaned up, the grades are up, the
body is different, the level is beaten, the low season lifts.

**The picture being offered:** permission runs the other direction. It shows up in the water, in
the not-yet, in the unfinished moment itself — spiritually baptizing the ordinary, incomplete thing
exactly as it is, without needing to resolve it first, and without giving up on the hope that it
will still change.

**Anchor citations:**
- "Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness." — Matthew 3:15, KJV
- "And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." —
  Matthew 3:16–17, KJV
- "Baptism. Purification by Spirit; submergence in Spirit." — Science and Health with Key to the
  Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 581
- "Christ. The divine manifestation of God, which comes to the flesh to destroy incarnate error."
  — Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 583
- "God is love." — 1 John 4:8, KJV
- "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me." — John 14:6, KJV

## Learning objective family

1. **Notice** the belief that rest, love, or belonging is something to re-earn once a hard or
   unfinished thing gets fixed.
2. **Feel**, not just understand intellectually, that permission and belovedness are already true,
   right now, in the middle of the unfinished thing — not contingent on finishing it.
3. **Carry** a short, sayable phrase ("suffer it to be so, now," or its age-appropriate equivalent)
   into one real unfinished moment this week.
4. **Locate** one real, current "not-yet" in their own life to actually say it over.

## The bridge, by age band

| Age band | What "earn it later" looks like to them | Bridge metaphor used |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | A drawing that isn't colored in yet, a tower still being built | Being told "I love watching you build that" partway through, not just once it's done |
| 8-11 | A video game level not yet beaten, a rough draft, a reading chart not yet full | The rough draft still counts vs. waiting for the "final" version to be allowed to feel good about it |
| 11-14 | Grades, body image, comparison, waiting to feel okay about yourself until something changes | Permission before the fix — fully known and already loved before anything gets resolved, not after |
| 14-21 | First jobs, dating, leaving home, not having life figured out yet | The water, not the finish line — belovedness shows up mid-transition, not once you've "arrived" |
| 21-plus | Career, marriage, parenting, an ordinary hard or low stretch (energy, mood, body, a season that hasn't lifted) | Spiritually baptizing the actual moment you're in — permitted for today, without waiting for the stretch to resolve first |

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **The formal vocabulary of "spiritual baptism," "the Christ," and the two Science and Health
  Glossary citations** (Baptism, Christ) — stays out of 5-8 and 8-11 entirely. Those bands get the
  Bible story and the "beloved" line in plain words, no doctrinal terms attached.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion questions** — never below 11-14; 11-14 offers a
  private-reflection option before any sharing.
- **A spoken-aloud "moral" or wrap-up line** — never for 5-8, rare for 8-11; the story and the
  craft carry it.
- **Career, marriage, parenting, and any general "hard season" material** (low energy, mood, body,
  a stretch that hasn't lifted) — 21-plus only, and always in generalized, non-diagnostic language.
  No named health condition, no addiction/recovery language — nothing more specific than "a long,
  low stretch" or "a hard season in your body or your energy."
- **John 14:6** — introduced starting at 14-21, to support the idea that "the way, the truth, and
  the life" isn't reserved for people who've already arrived; not needed at younger bands, where
  the point is carried through story alone.

## Facilitator-coaching principle

Below 14 or so, assume the volunteer teacher has less practice holding open discussion than the
14-21/21-plus material assumes — coach them in the moment for younger bands (what to say if a kid
asks a direct question), and hand over more discussion latitude at 14 and up.
