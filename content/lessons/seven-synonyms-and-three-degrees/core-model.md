# Lesson Core Model: "Seven Names, One Journey"

*Shared scaffolding for "Seven Synonyms & Three Degrees" — never taught directly to any age group.
Read this before writing or teaching any band's version. Two ideas anchor this lesson: Christian
Science uses seven names for God, and it describes the ordinary human journey toward understanding
as moving through three degrees. See BUILD-BRIEF.md section 6.2 for the confirmed scoping: the
5–8 version covers only the seven-names idea and drops three degrees entirely for that band alone.*

## The spiritual core (constant across all ages)

**Central claim:** God is knowable through seven real, complete names — Mind, Spirit, Soul,
Principle, Life, Truth, Love — not seven gods and not seven separate attributes, but seven full
doors into the same one God. And the ordinary human journey toward understanding and healing moves
through three degrees: struggle, effort, and understanding — a movement everyone makes, repeatedly,
not a permanent verdict on who they are.

**The picture being replaced:** God as one narrow, hard-to-relate-to label, and a person's own
struggle as evidence something is wrong with them rather than a normal, moving point on a shared
journey.

**The picture being offered:** God as generously knowable through many true names at once, and a
hard day, a hard year, or a hard stretch of growing up as motion on a journey everyone is on — not a
grade, not a verdict, not permanent.

**Anchor citations (used at whatever depth fits the age band):**
- "God is incorporeal, divine, supreme, infinite Mind, Spirit, Soul, Principle, Life, Truth, Love."
  — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 465
- The three degrees passage — Science and Health, p. 115: First Degree (Depravity — physical: evil
  beliefs, passions, fear, pride, envy, hatred); Second Degree (Evil beliefs disappearing — moral:
  honesty, affection, compassion, hope, faith); Third Degree (Understanding — spiritual: wisdom,
  purity, spiritual power, love, health, holiness).
- "In the third degree mortal mind disappears, and man as God's reflection appears... 'The last
  shall be first, and the first last.'" — Science and Health, p. 115
- "God is Love. Can we ask Him to be more?" — Science and Health, p. 2:23
- "Love is Principle, not person." — No and Yes, Mary Baker Eddy

## Learning objective family

1. **Notice** that God can be understood through more than one true name — and that this makes God
   more knowable, not more confusing.
2. **Feel** that a hard moment isn't a verdict on who they are — it's a normal, moving point on a
   journey everyone is on, including the adults around them.
3. **Carry** one name (of the seven), or one plain-language version of the journey, into a real
   moment this week.
4. **Locate** one real moment in their own life somewhere on the struggle-toward-understanding
   journey, without judgment.

## The bridge metaphor, by age band

| Age band | Seven-names bridge | Three-degrees bridge |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | A grown-up they love who is Mommy *and* Doctor *and* Coach *and* Chef — one whole person, every name true at once | Not taught at this band (6.2) |
| 8-11 | A multi-tool: one tool, several real jobs, each one fully real and useful | Learning to ride a bike: wobbly and scared, then getting the hang of it with practice, then riding without having to think about it |
| 11-14 | A real friend who is your study partner *and* your comic relief *and* the person you vent to *and* your competition in one class — still one real friend | The arc of a real blow-up: losing it, then working hard to hold it together, then actually being okay — not because you forced it, but because you understand something now |
| 14-21 / 21-plus | Existing general-audience version (self-guided or group) — broad enough to serve both bands for now. Splitting it further into distinctly-pitched teen and adult versions is a good next step whenever there's time to write it. | Same document; no split yet |

## What does NOT get inherited across age bands

- **Three degrees, at 5–8.** Dropped entirely for this band alone, per BUILD-BRIEF.md 6.2 — not
  simplified, not hinted at, just absent. The seven-names idea carries the whole lesson at this age.
- **Apologetics-style discussion** ("isn't that polytheism," "why seven, isn't one enough") belongs
  to 11–14 and up. Younger bands experience the idea through story; they don't need to defend it.
- **Naming a specific personal struggle out loud** as a requirement. Every band should offer a
  private or low-pressure way to locate a "first degree" moment rather than demanding one be spoken.

## Facilitator-coaching principle

The seven names and the three degrees are both easy to accidentally teach as a memorization task —
resist that at every age. The goal is a felt, usable sense of "God is bigger than one name" and "a
hard day doesn't mean something is wrong with me," not a quiz on vocabulary.
