## What you should know
At this age, being "the third person" is a daily experience: two friends fall out, the group chat catches fire, and you're holding a phone full of screenshots, loving both and not knowing what your thumbs should do. The usual options are bad (pick a side, go silent, send the fake-nice "you guys, stop!"), and the usual guilt is "I should have done something." This lesson gives them a repeatable move that isn't any of those.

Two things to watch for. First, many kids this age help *in order to be seen helping* (the scoreboard), and a lesson that says "be kind!" just adds pressure. The "Cup or Scoreboard?" beat is how we gently name that without shaming anyone. Second, quiet is often engagement at this age, not disinterest. Always offer a private-write option before any sharing.

This is the practice-sized follow-up to [Rescue or Faith?](/lessons/rescue-or-faith), and pairs well with [Finding the Bigger "Us"](/lessons/finding-the-bigger-us). No required prep.

## What we're assuming
- Everyone in the room has been the third person in somebody else's fight, and probably also one of the two.
- Faith backgrounds vary. We use "God" and "Love" plainly. Nobody is asked to agree.
- Some kids are in a real, current fight or being left out. They may not say so. Private writing is a real option.
- This is not the moment to talk about any particular issue the kids might be fighting over. The case is deliberately side-less.

## Learning goals
1. Recognize the two default moves (freeze/lecture, or rescue until you're drained) and that neither one is the only option.
2. Learn a four-step practice: **name the fear, spot the story, find the middle, do the cup.**
3. Be able to tell the difference between a cup and a scoreboard in their own actions.
4. Write one steady sentence of their own ("whatever's happening, what's true is...") and choose one real cup for this week.

## How to hold the room
- Open with the ground rules and mean them: think first, anyone may pass, no names. Say "no names" twice.
- Let silence sit. Rephrase once, then wait again.
- Do one out-loud "How clear is it, 1 to 5?" check-in (fingers held to the chest) after Beat 3 and say out loud what you'll adjust. Kids this age notice when a leader actually changes something because of what they said.
- Never let the case turn into a debate about the thing the two fictional friends are disagreeing about. If it does, go back to step one.

## The lesson flow
### Beat 1 (~6 min) — The cup on the table
Before the group arrives, place a small cup of water in the middle of the table. Don't explain it. Welcome everyone. Then say: "Think of a time two people you care about were fighting, or someone was really hurting, and you froze, or overdid it, or ghosted. Don't write names. Jot a few words on paper just for you." Give a minute of silent writing. Then: "Does anyone want to say, in a few words, which it usually is: freeze, over-fix, or disappear?" Take two or three. Anyone may pass.

### Beat 2 (~8 min) — The two hands
Draw two hands on the board.

- **The left hand** says the true thing: "God is All. Nobody is really broken. It's all fine." (The *center*, held alone.)
- **The right hand** does the kind thing: shows up, brings food, sits there, sends the text. (The *cup*, given alone.)

Say: "Alone, each one fails in its own way. The left hand alone is correct, and nobody can feel it. 'It's all fine!' said to a crying friend is a slammed door. The right hand alone gives and gives until it's empty, and it runs on fear. 'If I don't fix this, it's on me.' We want both hands in one hand."

Show the wheel on the board: **rim = cup, hub = center.** "The rim touches the road. The hub doesn't move. A wheel needs both." Read:

> "Be still, and know that I am God." — Psalm 46:10

Ask (private-write first, then optional share): "Which hand is your default?"

### Beat 3 (~10 min) — The four steps, live
Put the four steps on the board.

1. **Name the fear.** What's the fear here, humanly? Say it plainly. Naming it isn't dwelling; it's meeting.
2. **Spot the story.** What's the picture in your head? ("They're enemies now." "I have to pick." "It'll never be fixed.") It's a picture. It's not the truth about either of them.
3. **Find the middle.** What's true about both of them, whatever happened? It has to be true of both at once, and it can't need a loser.
4. **Do the cup.** One small kind thing, specific, possible, something you'd do for either of them.

Then read the case aloud, once:

*"Two friends you love, Nico and Sam, fell out. Nico left Sam out of a plan, and Sam found out. By tonight the group chat is on fire: screenshots, 'you always,' people choosing sides. You weren't in it. You've seen all of it. Both of them are your friends."*

**You** (the facilitator) model steps one and two out loud, tentatively. "Here's how it might look. The fear in me is: 'If I say anything I'll make it worse, and if I don't, I'm a bad friend.' The story in my head is: 'This is a disaster, and I'm the only one who can fix it.' That's a picture. It's not the truth about Nico or Sam."

Then groups of three do steps three and four on the case, about four minutes each. Walk around and ask only questions: "Is that true of both at once? Does it need a loser? Is the cup small enough to do tonight?" If a group starts choosing sides, send them back to step one.

*How-clear check-in:* "On a scale of 1 to 5, how clear is it? One means not yet clear. Five means you could explain it to someone." Fingers on chests, then say what you'll adjust.

### Beat 4 (~10 min) — Cup or Scoreboard?
Hand each trio the card sort (see printable). It lists twelve acts. For each, they decide: **Cup** (done for the person) or **Scoreboard** (done to be thanked, to win, to be seen as the good one, or to make sure nobody blames you). Many will be "it depends." Say: "That's the answer. Same act, different thought behind it. That's why the thought comes first."

Give them one test to carry:

"Would I give this same cup, with the same warmth, to the person who's hardest for me to love?"

Read:

> "Love is impartial and universal in its adaptation and bestowals. It is the open fount which cries, 'Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters.'" — Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 13:2–4

Add: "'Every one.' That includes the person you wouldn't pick."

### Beat 5 (~8 min) — Write a steady sentence
"Step three is the hardest, because the middle is easy to say and hard to mean. Let's write one that's yours." On an index card: *Whatever's happening, what's true is...* They write three versions fast, no editing, then circle the one they'd say, quietly and without an opponent, to a friend who's scared. Check it against four tests (on the handout): **present tense; plain words; true of everyone at once; still true on the worst day.** Offer three ways in: begin from God as All and everyone as loved; begin from Paul's promise below; begin from something you've proved yourself.

> "Neither death nor life... nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God." — Romans 8:38–39

Pairs: one reads, the other says only "It held when..." or "It wobbled when..."

### Beat 6 (~3 min) — A cup for the road
Each kid writes one real, small cup they could do this week on the back of the card ("ask Jordan how it's going, no agenda"). Bring the water back. Everyone pours one for a neighbor by name. "Would you like a cup of cold water?" No wrap-up speech. "See you next time."

## If it's not going well
Silence is normal; let it sit, rephrase once, wait again. Pushback ("this is dumb," "you can't be neutral," "what if someone is actually in the wrong?") is welcome engagement: curiosity beats correction. You can say: "Yes. And the cup isn't about deciding who's right. It's about the next small thing." A strong reaction (tears, anger, shutdown) means pausing, grounding the room with a breath or a stretch, and checking in with that student privately. If a student discloses self-harm, abuse, being unsafe, or anything urgent, stop facilitating a lesson, respond as a caring adult (stay calm, thank them, don't promise secrecy), and move to the site's mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page) and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations. This lesson's script stops there.
