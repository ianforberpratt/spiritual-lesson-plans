# Lesson Core Model: "The Cup and the Center"

*Shared scaffolding — never taught directly to any age group. Every band file is checked against this, not against each other. Companion lessons: "Rescue or Faith?" (why you're helping), "Seven Names, One Journey" (the degrees), "Finding the Bigger Us" and "When Someone Feels Like the Enemy" (conflict).*

## The spiritual core (constant across all ages)

**Central claim:** Two things that feel like they compete belong in one hand: the **center** (what is true whatever appears: God is All, and every person is God's own spiritual image, whole and loved, never broken) and the **cup** (one small, specific, warm act of love, given in a spirit and not for a reward). A cup without a center runs out. A center without a cup is correctness nobody can feel. One repeatable sequence carries both: **address the fear, notice what appears in thought, turn to the center, choose the cup.**

**The picture being replaced:** love as an either/or. Either you hold the truth (and your hands forget the person in front of you), or you pour yourself out for the person (and fear decides how, and you run dry). Underneath both: "I have to feel certain before I can help," and "if I don't fix this, it's on me."

**The picture being offered:** a wheel. The rim touches the road and bumps along, muddy and busy and real. The hub never moves, and the whole wheel turns on it. You can be wobbly on the outside and still in the middle. Fear gets named first (never skipped, never obeyed), the picture in thought is seen as a picture and not as the truth about anyone, the center is held, and then a small hand reaches out.

## Learning objective family

1. **Notice** that fear shows up first, and that it pushes us toward one of two bad moves: freezing (or lecturing) or over-rescuing.
2. **Feel** that the center doesn't move when circumstances do, and that being wobbly on the outside is not the same as having lost the middle.
3. **Carry** a four-move practice into a real moment this week, small enough to do on a Monday.
4. **Locate** one real, current, low-stakes situation to practice it on, without naming names.

## The bridge metaphor, by age band

| Band | The real daily scene | The bridge |
|---|---|---|
| 5-8 | A friend's block tower or sandcastle falls and they cry | A wagon wheel (wobbly outside, still in the middle) and a real little cup of water |
| 8-11 | A teammate misses the last shot and everyone goes quiet | A bike wheel (rim in the mud, hub holds) and a water bottle passed down the bench |
| 11-14 | The group chat blows up between two friends and you only see the screenshots | The third seat: you love both, you weren't in it, and you have to decide what to do with your thumbs |
| 14-21 | A roommate fight, a group-project meltdown, a friend's 1 a.m. text, a scary headline | The same wheel, plus the three degrees as a map of what appears in thought, and a one-sentence center you write yourself |
| 21+ | The dinner table, a diagnosis, a hard conversation, the news, the person you can't forgive | "Holding the Absolute and the casserole": the two hands, the five tests for a center statement, and the three seats of Luke 15 |

## What does NOT get inherited across bands

- **The three degrees, 5-8:** absent entirely (same rule as Seven Names). 8-11: only as plain "three kinds of weather" (wobbly, working at it, got it), never with the book's vocabulary.
- **The book's degree vocabulary** (depravity, hatred, revenge, sin, sickness, death): 14-21 and 21+ only, always with the sentence "the map describes what appears in thought; it does not rank persons." Never below 14.
- **The word "treatment"** and any illness framing: 21+ only. Younger bands say "prayer" or just "the four moves."
- **Diagnosis, bills, marriage, workplace, and "someone you can't forgive":** 21+ only. 14-21 gets independence-era scenes (roommates, group projects, first jobs, a friend in crisis).
- **News headlines as a practice case:** 14-21 and 21+ only, with a "public sorrow, no sides" rule and no distressing imagery.
- **Open-ended abstract discussion questions:** never below 11. 5-8 and 8-11 learn by doing and by pairs.
- **A spoken "moral" at the end:** never for 5-8, rarely for 8-11. The cup of water carries it.
- **Naming a real person or a real conflict out loud:** never required in any band. Every sharing beat has a private-write or pass option.

## Case rules (all bands)
Every case is deliberately side-less. The facilitator never names the issue, never takes a side, and sends any group that starts arguing the issue back to step one. The practice matters more than any one question.

## Facilitator-coaching principle
Coach, don't script. Tell a volunteer teacher *why* a beat works. The facilitator models steps one and two out loud, *tentatively* ("here's how it might look"), and the learners do steps three and four. A "not yet" is a welcome answer. Pushback is the lesson working.
