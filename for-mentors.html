For Mentors

You don't need training. You need a way in.

These lessons are built so a first-time mentor and an experienced one can both lead them well. Here's how they work, and how to hold the room.

The approach

Built on adult-learning pedagogy — for young people

That sounds backwards, but it isn't. Andragogy (adult learning theory) treats the learner as someone who already has experience worth drawing on, who learns by doing rather than being told, and who needs to see relevance before they'll engage. Gen Z and Gen Alpha research says the same thing about younger learners: they disengage from lecture, and they lean in when a topic feels personally real and they have some control over how they explore it.

So every lesson here follows the same shape:

  • Start from experience. Open with a question about something the young person has already lived, not a definition.
  • Make it active. A short activity, discussion prompt, or reflection — never just reading at them.
  • Leave room for their own answer. The goal is never to arrive at the "right" response. It's to help them trust their own thinking.
  • Close with the premise. Every lesson returns to the same anchor: you are already whole.

Before you start

A few things worth knowing

  • You don't need a religious background or formal training to lead one of these. Read it once beforehand, and you're ready.
  • These lessons are rooted in Christian Science, but written to welcome any faith background in the room. You're not asked to convert anyone — just to hold space.
  • There's no wrong way to adapt a lesson to your group. Skip a section, slow down, or sit with silence — the structure is a guide, not a script.
  • If a young person pushes back on an idea, that's the lesson working, not failing. Curiosity and doubt are welcome here.

Facilitation basics

Holding the room well

01

Ask more than you tell

Every lesson includes discussion prompts. Resist the urge to fill silence — a pause is often the young person doing real thinking, not being stuck.

02

Meet them without judgment

Whatever a young person brings — doubt, a different faith, a hard question — the response is curiosity, not correction. That's the whole premise of this resource.

03

Make it theirs

Adapt language, examples, and pacing to your group. A lesson that's been reshaped to fit the room in front of you is being used exactly as intended.
