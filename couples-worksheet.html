How to read it

  • The middle is shared. A dot toward one side means that person carries more of that area. It does not need to be 50/50 everywhere. What matters is whether it feels fair to both of you.
  • This is a role, never a worth. Overfunctioning is doing more than your share, often out of love, worry, or habit. Underfunctioning is doing less than you could, often because the other person already covers it. The two feed each other, and nobody planned it.
  • The quiet kind counts. If one of you leans heavily in Feeling, that person may be going along to keep the peace. It can look like harmony and still be costly.
  • Two different pictures are not a mistake. Research on couples finds partners often see the split differently. A gap between your dots is the best place to start talking.

Talk about it for ten minutes

  1. Each finish this sentence out loud, and only listen: “The thing I carry that nobody sees is ______.”
  2. Look at where your two dots are farthest apart. Ask with real curiosity: “What do you see that I don’t?”
  3. Pick one experiment below and put it on the calendar.

What is this based on?

This check-in borrows formats and ideas from published research on who does the work in couples, so the questions are not made up from scratch:

  • The five-step “who carries it” scale and the fairness question follow formats used in household-labor research (Blair and Lichter’s fairness items, described in Le and Aune, 2011).
  • The Thinking and Feeling areas follow research on the “mental load”: cognitive labor (anticipating, deciding, following up) and emotional labor, and how tired people feel carrying them (see Barigozzi and colleagues, 2025). That work also found that partners often disagree about who organizes what.
  • The words overfunctioning and underfunctioning come from Bowen family systems theory, and reached us through the Reimagining Love podcast with Dr. Alexandra Solomon.

What it is not. It is not a validated clinical test. We did not find a published scale that measures overfunctioning itself in couples, so the picture is a simple summary of your own ticks, not a score compared to other people. It cannot diagnose anything. Use it to start a kind, curious conversation.
