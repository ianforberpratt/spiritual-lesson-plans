The Cup Menu by Degree

Step two names what appears in thought. The cup that fits depends on it.

FIRST DEGREEwhen it’s fear, hurt, angerTHE CUP IS SAFETY
  • Stay in the room. Say little.
  • Text: “I’m here. No need to answer.”
  • Sit in the car with them until they’re ready.
  • Bring food or water.
  • Say their name calmly and nothing else.
  • Stay steady yourself: breathe; don’t match their volume.
SECOND DEGREEwhen they’re trying, getting honestTHE CUP IS ENCOURAGEMENT
  • “That took guts to say.”
  • Name one thing they did right this week.
  • Take one small task off their plate.
  • Ask what they’ve already tried.
  • Give a short, honest apology with no “but.”
  • Remind them of a time they came through.
THIRD DEGREEwhen they’re steady and wonderingTHE CUP IS CLARITY
  • Say the plain true thing once, kindly, then stop.
  • Offer a passage with no pressure: “This helped me.”
  • Ask “What do you think is true here?” and listen.
  • Help them see the whole picture, not just the screenshot.
  • Say what you see in them: “You’re not what happened.”
  • Pray specifically and quietly. Tell no one.
The map describes what appears in thought. It does not rank persons. The center comes first. Then the cup.
