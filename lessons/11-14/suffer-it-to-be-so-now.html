A lesson · Permission Comes First

Grade 6-8

Permission Comes First

You don’t have to fix yourself first to be worth loving — that part was never actually in question.

Time35-40 min
Groupsmall-group or full class (4-16 kids), private-reflection option first
AgeGrade 6-8
Formatin-person or virtual
UpdatedAugust 30, 2026
Also built for:K-2Grade 3-5TeenAge 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

This is the age where the belief “I’ll feel okay about myself once ___ changes” gets loudest — grades, body, followers, being picked or not picked, comparison with friends. Abstract thinking is online enough now to actually name that pattern out loud, but self-worth is unusually externally anchored at this age, so keep this closer to felt experience than to theory, and offer privacy before any sharing. Nothing you don’t already have for prep beyond the printable.

What we're assuming

- Most kids this age already have at least one real “once ___ happens, I’ll feel okay about myself” belief running quietly in the background. - Self-worth is genuinely more fragile and externally anchored at this age than either younger or older groups — treat the topic with real care, not just as an interesting idea. - This room holds a range of faith backgrounds and starting points; nobody has to share anything they’re not ready to. - Give a private-reflection option before asking anyone to share out loud — forced vulnerability at this age often produces performance, not honesty.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

1. Name, specifically, one thing they’re currently waiting to “fix” before letting themselves feel okay, loved, or at peace. 2. Understand the real difference between permission that has to be earned and permission that was already given — tested against a real moment, not just a slogan. 3. Carry one question into the week: “Am I waiting for something to be fixed before I let myself feel loved, right now?” 4. Have at least one private, written reflection they don’t have to share with anyone.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

- Don’t require sharing. Offer it, make it easy to pass, and let silence be okay. - If a kid pushes back with something like “but grades actually do matter” — they’re right, and that’s not the point being challenged. The point is narrower: whether a person’s worth is the same thing as their performance. Hold both truths rather than arguing one away. - Watch for a kid who goes quieter than usual — check in one-on-one after, not in the moment.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~5 min

Naming the “once ___” belief

Ask (open floor, or written on paper if the group is shy): “Is there something you’re quietly waiting to fix, finish, or fix about yourself before you’ll let yourself feel okay, or feel loved? Grades, how you look, a friendship, a mistake you’re still carrying — you don’t have to say which one out loud.”

2
~8 min

The story, in full

Tell the story of Matthew 3:13–17: John baptizing people in the Jordan, calling them to turn their lives around; Jesus arriving and asking for the same baptism; John balking — “I have need to be baptized of thee” — essentially, this should be the other way around. Read together:

Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.

Matthew 3:15, KJV

”‘Suffer,’ here, doesn’t mean ‘endure something hard.’ It means let it be. Allow it. Permit it.”

And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

Matthew 3:16–17, KJV

“Jesus hadn’t done any of his well-known work yet — no teaching people had heard, no healing, nothing. And the very first thing that happens, in the water, before any of it, is: he’s named beloved.”

Discuss: “Why do you think the belovedness came before the work started, instead of after?”

3
~7 min

Reading it plainly

Read together:

Baptism. Purification by Spirit; submergence in Spirit.

Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 581

“Not something you complete — something you’re already immersed in. Like water that’s already all around you.”

God is love.

1 John 4:8, KJV

“Not ‘God will love you once you’ve fixed the thing.’ God’s nature already is love — full stop, no waiting period.”

Discuss: “What would it mean to actually believe that the fixing isn’t a requirement for the loving?”

4
~7 min

Private reflection

Hand out paper. Not shared aloud unless a student chooses to.

Prompt: “What are you currently waiting to finish, fix, or improve before you’ll let yourself feel loved or okay? What would change if that permission came first, instead of last?”

Give real quiet — 3–4 minutes.

5
~6 min

The question to carry

Teach: “Am I waiting for something to be fixed before I let myself feel loved, right now?”

“This week, notice one moment — a grade, a comparison, an argument, a hard morning — and try asking yourself that question honestly, not impressively.”

6

Closing

“You are not on hold. Whatever you’re in the middle of fixing, that was never the price of being loved.”

What you'll need

The materials

  • Paper and pens for each participant
  • The “Permission Comes First” wallet card

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

- Silence is normal here — let it sit. - Pushback or disagreement (“but I do have to earn things”) is welcome, not a problem — it’s often the lesson working. - A strong emotional reaction — pause, ground the room, check in privately. - A disclosure of real harm — self-harm, abuse, or anything beyond ordinary middle-school pressure — stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, and follow the site’s mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations. This can arrive quietly, inside a written reflection rather than spoken aloud — take it just as seriously.

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
