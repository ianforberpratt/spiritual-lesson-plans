A lesson · Doors or Walls: Finding Words That Fit

Middle School (Grades 6–8)

Doors or Walls: Finding Words That Fit

Some words about God make you want to lean in. Some make you want to leave the room. Both are worth noticing.

Time35–45 min
Group1:1 or group
AgeMiddle School (Grades 6–8)
Formatin-person or virtual
Also built for:14–21 · Teen & Early College21+ · College Student & Adult (21+)

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

This is often the first time a young person consciously notices that a word an adult used for God doesn’t quite land anymore — without yet having language for why. That’s not doubt to be corrected; it’s a normal, healthy part of abstract thinking coming online around this age. Don’t supply the “right” word, and don’t treat a kid’s honest “that word doesn’t work for me anymore” as a problem to fix. The private journaling step exists so kids who aren’t ready to say something out loud still get to think it through — never require anyone to share what they wrote.

What we're assuming

  • Everyone in the room already has some vocabulary for “something bigger than me” — even “I don’t know” counts.
  • Some kids may feel protective of words their family or church uses, and some may already feel quiet friction with those words. Both are welcome here.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

  1. Notices at least one word about God or the sacred that feels like a door, and one that feels like a wall — for them, specifically.
  2. Writes down, privately, one word they’re unsure still fits, without pressure to explain it to anyone.
  3. Tries translating one word into their own language, keeping what the original word was trying to protect.
  4. Leaves knowing that a word not working for them isn’t the same as the idea being wrong — and that no one else gets to decide the “right” word for them.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

  • Keep the tone light and curious, not confessional — this isn’t a place to relitigate family or church doctrine out loud.
  • Offer the gentler starter words (believe, pray, holy) first. Only offer the heavier ones (master, worship, sin) as an option, not a requirement.
  • Never call on someone to share their private journal entry. Ask for volunteers only.
  • If a kid says a word connected to their family or church doesn’t work for them, don’t reassure them out of it or defend the word. Just let it be noticed.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~8 min

Words That Land, Words That Don’t

Name a few words adults have used around you to talk about God or “something bigger” — church, family, books, movies, wherever. For each one: does it feel like a door you want to walk through, or a wall you want to walk around?

2
~8 min

The Idea Isn’t the Words

Share: people made up the words we use for God, a long time ago and more recently too, doing their best to point at something too big for words to fully hold. Words can wear out or stop fitting. What they’re pointing at doesn’t wear out. Discuss: Is there a word your family or tradition uses for God that still feels true to you? Is there one that used to feel true and doesn’t quite anymore?

3
~5 min

Private Journal: Your Word

On your own, write down one word about God or the sacred that you’re not sure fits you anymore — or one that still fits and you want to hold onto. This is just for you. No one will ask you to read it out loud.

4
~10 min

Translate It

Pick one word — believe, pray, holy, or (if you want) master, worship, sin — and try putting it in your own words, without losing what the original word was trying to protect. Example: “believe” might become “trusting something even when I can’t see all of it yet.” Discuss (share only if you want to): What did you land on? What was tricky about translating it?

5
~5–8 min

You Get to Keep Translating

Close with: there’s no test here, and no wrong word. Whatever words you have right now — or don’t have yet — for something bigger than you that loves you, that’s enough for today. You get to keep translating as you grow.

What you'll need

The materials

  • Paper and pens, or index cards for the private journaling beat — a shared doc works for virtual groups, but keep the journaling portion private (not shared on screen)

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

At this age, what surfaces is often quiet rather than dramatic — a kid going silent, seeming embarrassed, or worrying they’re disappointing a parent or church by admitting a word doesn’t work for them anymore. Don’t push for more. Reassure them that noticing is allowed and nothing here needs to leave the room unless they want it to. If something surfaces that feels bigger than this lesson — real family conflict, fear, or distress — pause and follow the site’s mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page).

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
