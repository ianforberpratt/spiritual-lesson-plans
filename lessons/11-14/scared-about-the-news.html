A lesson · Not the Whole Story

Grade 6-8

Not the Whole Story

The scroll doesn’t get the final word on who you are or what’s real.

Time35-40 min
Groupsmall group or full class (4-16 kids), private-reflection option first
AgeGrade 6-8
Formatin-person or virtual
UpdatedSeptember 4, 2026
Also built for:K-2Grade 3-5TeenAge 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

This is the age where a phone or a feed often becomes a genuinely new source of scary information — kids are seeing things now that they weren’t a couple years ago, right as self-worth is unusually externally anchored. Abstract thinking is coming online enough to name the pattern, but keep it closer to felt experience than theory, and offer a private-reflection option before any sharing. Nothing you don’t already have for prep beyond the printable.

What we're assuming

  • Most kids this age have already seen something on a phone or feed that scared or upset them, whether or not they’d say so out loud.
  • This room holds a range of faith backgrounds and starting points — nobody has to share anything they’re not ready to.
  • Quiet is often engagement here, not disinterest — don’t read silence as failure.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

  • Name, specifically, one thing on their feed or in the news that’s actually scared them lately.
  • Understand the real difference between a feeling being real and a feeling being the whole truth.
  • Carry one repeatable question into the week for the next time something scary crosses their feed.
  • Have at least one private, written reflection they don’t have to share with anyone.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

  • Offer private reflection before any group sharing — forced vulnerability at this age often produces performance, not honesty.
  • If a kid pushes back with “but this stuff is actually scary” — they’re right, and that’s not the point being challenged. The point is narrower: whether the scary thing gets to define who they are or what’s ultimately true.
  • Watch for a kid who goes quiet in a different way than usual — check in one-on-one after, not in the moment.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~6 min

Naming it

Private reflection first (paper, not shared aloud unless chosen): “What’s one thing you’ve seen or heard lately — on a phone, online, from a friend, anywhere — that actually scared you?”

Give real quiet — 2-3 minutes.

2
~8 min

Real, but not the whole story

Say: “That thing you just named is real. Nobody’s telling you it isn’t, or that you shouldn’t feel however you feel about it. But here’s a question worth sitting with: does that one scary thing get to be the whole story about you, or about the world?”

Read together:

There is no fear in Love, but perfect Love casteth out fear... He that feareth is not made perfect in Love.

1 John 4:18, KJV

Discuss (whole group or small groups, private-reflection kids can pass): “This doesn’t say the fear isn’t real. What do you think it’s actually saying fear doesn’t get to do?”

3
~9 min

Division is part of the noise

Say: “A lot of what scares people this age isn’t just disasters — it’s the sense that everybody’s picking sides, that people can’t agree on anything, that the world feels split in half.”

Read together:

There is neither Jew nor Gentile, neither slave nor free, nor is there male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

Galatians 3:28, NIV

Discuss: “This was written to a world that also felt split into hard categories. What do you think it’s actually claiming is true underneath all that division?”

4
~9 min

Choosing which fire to feed

Say: “When something scares us, it’s really easy for that fear to turn into anger at somebody — a comment, an argument, a group chat blowup. There’s an old skill for this.”

Read together:

A person’s wisdom yields patience; it is to one’s glory to overlook an offense.

Proverbs 19:11, NIV

Say: “That’s not the same as staying silent about something wrong. It’s choosing which fire you feed — the scared-and-lashing-out fire, or a calmer one.”

Discuss: “What’s one time you’ve felt a scared feeling turn into anger at someone who didn’t actually deserve it?”

5
~6 min

What to carry

Teach the question: “Is this scary thing the whole story, or is it one loud part of a bigger true thing about who I am?”

Private, unshared writing prompt to close: “One thing I want to remember next time my feed scares me:”

What you'll need

The materials

Full handout The Storm Isn’t You A scary feeling passes through like weather. It was never the whole sky — and it was never you. Open printable handout →

Paper for private reflection (kept private unless a student chooses to share) and the printable handout — nothing else required.

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

  • Silence after a discussion question: Normal at this age — let it sit; don’t fill it for them.
  • Pushback/disagreement: Welcome, not a problem — see Beat 2 and Beat 4 notes above.
  • A strong emotional reaction: Pause, ground the room, check in with that student privately.
  • A disclosure of real harm: Stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, and point to the site’s mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations.

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
