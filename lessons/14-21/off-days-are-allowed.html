Off Days Are Allowed

Feeling disconnected doesn’t mean you’re doing it wrong.

Time40–55 min
Group1:1 or group
AgeTeen
FormatIn-person or virtual
UpdatedAugust 20, 2026
Also built for:K-2Grade 3-5Grade 6-8Age 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

Nothing required. If it helps to know where this came from: there’s a sneaky belief that shows up on hard days — “if I were doing this right, I wouldn’t feel this way.” This lesson pushes back on that belief directly, without pretending the hard day isn’t real.

What we're assuming

Everyone in the room has had an “off day” — low, disconnected, more human than usual. We’re not assuming anyone will describe theirs, or that it’s tied to anything specific. Off days don’t need a reason to be real.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

  • Leaves knowing that feeling disconnected, low, or “off” isn’t proof they’re doing something wrong.
  • Leaves able to name the difference between a real feeling and the guilt trap of grading themselves for having it.
  • Leaves having practiced saying a hard truth and a hopeful one in the same breath, without picking one to fake.
  • Leaves knowing off days don’t cost them their wholeness — it was never something they had to earn in the first place.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

  • This can surface real low mood, not just a rough afternoon. Watch for signs it’s more than “off” — see the safety note below.
  • Don’t rush to fix the feeling. The goal here is normalizing it, not curing it in one session.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~10 min

Naming an Off Day

Think of a recent day you felt “off” — disconnected, low, more human than usual. No shame in it. Just notice it happened, and that it’s not unusual.

2
~10–15 min

The Trap of Grading Yourself

Name the sneaky belief out loud: “if I were doing things right, I wouldn’t feel this way.” That belief is the actual problem — not the off day. Discuss: Have you ever felt guilty about feeling bad — like the feeling itself was proof you were failing at something?

3
~15 min

You’re Not Being Tested

You’re not a student being graded on how spiritual, positive, or put-together you’re managing to feel today. Being honest — “I know things are okay, but right now I feel scared” — isn’t a failure. It’s just honest, and both things can be true at once. Try it: Practice saying, out loud or on paper: “I know ______ is true, but right now I feel ______.”

4
~10 min

Information, Not Indictment

An off day isn’t a punishment or a sign you’re behind. Sometimes it’s just where you are today. Close with this: you don’t lose your wholeness on a bad day. It was never something you had to keep earning.

What you'll need

The materials

  • Paper and pens — or a shared doc / whiteboard if you’re meeting virtually
  • Optional printables: Handout “Both Things Are True”; Keepsake “Permission Slip”

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

Read this one before you lead the lesson, not during it: if “off days” sound like they’ve been going on for weeks, or come with hopelessness, losing interest in things they used to enjoy, or any mention of self-harm — this isn’t a spiritual-practice conversation anymore, it’s a wellbeing one. Don’t try to reframe it away. Take it seriously and involve the right support, per the site’s mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page).

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
