Words That Open Doors

Old language, old thoughts — and making the sacred make sense today.

Time40–55 min
Group1:1 or group
AgeTeen
Formatin-person or virtual
UpdatedAugust 25, 2026
Also built for:Grade 6-8Age 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

Nothing required. A mentor noticed that words like “master” or “leader” — words meant to point at something sacred — sometimes land as walls instead of doors for young people today. This lesson isn’t about fixing anyone’s vocabulary. It’s about noticing whether the words you inherited still work for you.

What we're assuming

  • Everyone in the room already has some vocabulary for “something bigger than me” — even if that vocabulary is “I don’t know yet” or “I don’t believe in anything.” That counts too.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

  1. Leaves knowing that the words we inherit for the sacred aren’t the sacred thing itself — you’re allowed to hold language loosely, even words your family or tradition handed you.
  2. Leaves having named, out loud or on paper, one word about spirituality that still feels true, and one that doesn’t work for them anymore.
  3. Leaves understanding you can respect where an idea came from without putting the person who said it on a pedestal.
  4. Leaves feeling like they have permission to find their own words, instead of only inheriting someone else’s.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

  • This gets personal fast. Some young people carry real hurt from religious language used against them. Say so up front.
  • There’s no “correct” replacement word. Resist the urge to supply one.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~10 min

Words That Land, Words That Don’t

List a few words adults have used around you to talk about God, the sacred, or “something bigger.” For each: does it feel like a door, or a wall?

2
~10–15 min

The Idea Isn’t the Words

Share: the words people use for the sacred were made by humans, at some point in history, doing their best to point at something bigger than words can hold. Words can get old. What they’re pointing at doesn’t. Discuss: Is there a word your family, culture, or tradition uses for “something greater than you” that still feels alive? Is there one that used to work and doesn’t anymore?

3
~15 min

Translate It

Pick one “old” word — master, leader, worship, sin, holy — and try translating it into words that feel true to you today, without losing what the original word was trying to protect. Example: “master” might become “someone who shows me a door, not someone who stands in it.”

4
~10 min

You Get to Keep Translating

Reflect: what’s the difference between respecting where an idea came from, and worshipping the person who said it? Close with: you don’t need the perfect words. Whatever words you use — or don’t have yet — for the power greater than yourself that loves you, that’s still true, right now.

What you'll need

The materials

  • Paper and pens — or a shared doc / whiteboard if you’re meeting virtually
  • Optional printables: Handout “Door or Wall?”; Handout “Translate It”

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

Talking about religious language is personal, and it can surface real pain — a falling-out with a church, a family conflict, spiritual hurt that hasn’t healed. That’s not a detour from this lesson. Slow down rather than rushing to the next beat. If something surfaces that’s beyond what the room can hold, use the site’s mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page).

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
