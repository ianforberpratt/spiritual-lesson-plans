The Water, Not the Finish Line

You don’t need your life figured out to be allowed to rest in it, right now.

Time40-45 min
Groupsmall-group or full group, near-adult discussion depth
AgeTeen
Formatin-person or virtual
UpdatedAugust 30, 2026
Also built for:K-2Grade 3-5Grade 6-8Age 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

This age is living the “once I ___” belief in its most independence-shaped form: once I have a job, once I know what I’m doing, once I look a certain way, once my life is more together, then I’ll deserve to rest, feel confident, or feel loved. Anchor every example in independence and identity — first jobs, dating, leaving home — not childhood or established-adult scenarios. This age can hold real theological vocabulary (spiritual baptism, the Christ) directly, unlike younger bands. Nothing you don’t already have for prep beyond the printable.

What we're assuming

- Most people this age are actively mid-transition in some real way — a first job, a relationship, moving out, figuring out who they are apart from family — and are quietly waiting to feel “arrived” before they’ll let themselves feel secure. - This room holds a range of faith backgrounds, including people with no religious background at all; the story and the idea are offered, not required. - Nobody has to share anything specific about their own transition to get something real out of this.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

1. Notice the specific “once I ___” belief currently running in their own life. 2. Feel, not just agree with intellectually, that belovedness doesn’t wait for a finished or “arrived” version of themselves. 3. Carry the phrase “permitted, for today” into one real moment of uncertainty this week. 4. Locate one real, current transition or unresolved thing to actually try it on.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

- Near-adult discussion depth is appropriate here — don’t shy from real uncertainty, real timelines, real fear about not having things figured out. - Stay curious rather than reassuring too fast. Let the discomfort of not having it figured out sit for a moment before offering the reframe. - If someone pushes back — “isn’t this just an excuse to not try” — that’s a real, fair question. The answer isn’t that trying doesn’t matter; it’s that trying and being loved were never actually sequenced.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~7 min

Naming the “once I...” belief

Ask: “What’s the ‘once I ___, then I’ll finally feel like I’ve got this’ sentence running in your head right now? Once I get the job. Once I move out for good. Once I know what I actually want. Once I look different. Name it, even just to yourself.”

Discuss: “Where do you think that sentence came from — did anyone actually teach it to you, or did it just accumulate?”

2
~10 min

The story, full depth

Tell Matthew 3:13–17 in full: John baptizing people in the Jordan as a public turning-around; Jesus arriving and asking for the same baptism; John’s confusion — he has nothing to wash off, so why does he need this? Jesus’s answer:

Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.

Matthew 3:15, KJV

“Suffer here means let it be. Allow it. Permit it — not endure something hard, the way we usually hear that word.”

And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

Matthew 3:16–17, KJV

“That happens before any of his teaching, before any healing, before anything people would later remember him for. The belovedness isn’t a review of finished work. It’s spoken over him in the water, at the very start.”

Discuss: “If you take away the idea that this baptism was about washing away something wrong, what’s actually happening here instead?”

3
~8 min

Spiritual baptism, the Christ, and where they meet you

Read together:

Baptism. Purification by Spirit; submergence in Spirit.

Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 581

“Not a ritual to complete — a full immersion in a Spirit that’s already surrounding you. You’re not working toward it. You’re already in it.”

Christ. The divine manifestation of God, which comes to the flesh to destroy incarnate error.

Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 583

God is love.

1 John 4:8, KJV

“If God is love, the Christ is love showing up in the actual, specific texture of a human life — including the unfinished, uncertain, mid-transition ones.”

I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

John 14:6, KJV

“The way, the truth, and the life — not reserved for people who’ve already arrived somewhere. Available exactly where you are right now.”

Discuss: “What would change if you actually believed the permission wasn’t waiting at the end of figuring your life out?”

4
~10 min

Where this gets real

Small groups: pick one real “once I ___” moment (a job search, a first apartment, a relationship still forming, a version of yourself this year you’re not thrilled to be) and talk through:

1. What are you actually waiting to happen before you’ll let yourself rest, or feel loved, or feel like a real adult? 2. What would it look like to say “suffer it to be so, now” over that exact thing — not giving up on it changing, just not making the change a condition for today?

5
~7 min

The experiment

Close with an invitation, not an assignment: this week, pick one real, current, unfinished thing and try saying — out loud, if you’re willing — “suffer it to be so, now.” Not forever. Not without hope that it changes. Just permitted, for today.

6

Closing

“The permission was never waiting at the finish line. It showed up in the water, before the ministry even started — and it’s showing up in yours, wherever you actually are right now.”

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

- Silence is normal and fine here — let it sit. - Pushback (“isn’t this just an excuse”) is a fair, real question, not a problem — engage it honestly rather than defending the idea. - A strong emotional reaction — someone realizes how long they’ve been withholding rest or love from themselves — pause, don’t rush past it, offer a private word after. - A disclosure of real harm — stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, and follow the site’s mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations.

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
