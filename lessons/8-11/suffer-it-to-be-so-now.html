A lesson · The Rough Draft Counts

Grade 3-5

The Rough Draft Counts

You don’t have to beat the level or finish the final draft to already count, right now.

Time30-35 min
Groupsmall-group or full class (4-20 kids)
AgeGrade 3-5
Formatin-person or virtual
UpdatedAugust 30, 2026
Also built for:K-2Grade 6-8TeenAge 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

This age knows exactly what it feels like to be mid-level, mid-draft, mid-anything — a video game not yet beaten, a book not yet finished, a reading chart not yet full. That’s the bridge: the difference between a rough draft and a “final” one, and whether a person’s allowed to feel good about something before it’s the finished version. Unlike the 5-8 version, this age can compare two things side by side and do simple discussion in pairs. Nothing you don’t already have for prep beyond the printable.

What we're assuming

- Most kids this age have a real, current example of something unfinished — a level, a project, a skill they’re still learning. - Some kids feel real pressure already around finishing things “right” — perfectionism, a sibling who finishes faster, grades starting to matter. Stay alert to a kid who seems more anxious than curious. - This isn’t a lesson about not trying hard, or about finishing things not mattering. It’s narrower: whether you’re allowed to feel okay right now, before it’s done.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

1. Name one real thing in their life right now that’s still “in progress.” 2. Understand — as an actual comparison, not just a phrase — the difference between waiting to feel good about something until it’s finished, and being allowed to feel good about it now, mid-draft. 3. Carry one phrase into the week: “This still counts, even now.” 4. Locate one real project, skill, or situation to try it on.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

- Let them do the comparing rather than telling them the answer — ask which one sounds more like “waiting to earn it” and which sounds like “already allowed,” and let them land on it. - Pairs of 2-3 work better than one big circle at this age for the discussion moments. - If the room gets silly comparing video games, ride it — it’s the exact right material for the lesson.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~5 min

Name something unfinished

Ask: “What’s something you’re in the middle of right now — a video game, a book, a project, a skill you’re still learning? What would it feel like if you weren’t allowed to feel good about it at all until it was totally finished?”

Let several kids share quickly, round-robin.

2
~7 min

The story

Tell or read aloud the Jordan River scene (John and Jesus), including the “suffer it to be so now” line and its plain meaning — “let it be, allow it, permit it” — plus the voice from heaven naming Jesus beloved right there in the water, before any of his famous work had started.

Ask (pairs, 2 min): “Jesus hadn’t done any of his big, well-known work yet — no miracles, nothing people had heard about. Why do you think the voice called him beloved right then, instead of waiting?”

3
~7 min

Rough draft vs. final draft

“Think about a rough draft of a story you’re writing, and the final, polished version. What’s actually still true about the rough draft, even though it’s not finished?” (It’s still yours. It still counts as real writing. It can still be good, even messy.)

Discuss in pairs: “Is there a rule in your head that says you’re only allowed to feel proud, or feel loved, once something is the ‘final’ version? Where do you think that rule came from?”

4
~7 min

Reading it plainly

Read together:

Baptism. Purification by Spirit; submergence in Spirit.

Science and Health, Mary Baker Eddy, p. 581

“In plain words: this is saying God’s love is like water that’s already all around you — you’re already in it. You don’t have to do anything first to be surrounded by it.”

God is love.

1 John 4:8

“Not ‘God loves people once they finish something.’ God’s whole nature already is love — like water is wet. It doesn’t wait.”

5
~6 min

Draw or write it

Give paper. Fold in half. One side: draw or write the “unfinished thing” from Beat 1. Other side: write “This still counts, even now” across it.

Pairs share if they’d like — not required.

6

Closing

“You don’t have to wait for the final draft to already count. You already do — right now, mid-level, mid-draft, exactly as you are today.”

What you'll need

The materials

Wallet card Permission Comes First You don’t have to wait for the finished version — the final draft, the fixed thing, the “better” you — to already count and already be loved. Open printable handout →
  • Paper (folded in half) and pencils or markers
  • The “Permission Comes First” wallet card

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

- Silence is fine — let it sit. - A kid seems more anxious than curious talking about being unfinished or “not good enough yet” — follow up individually after class rather than pressing in the group. - A strong emotional reaction — pause, ground the room, check in privately. - A disclosure of real harm — stop facilitating, respond as a caring adult, follow the site’s mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations.

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
