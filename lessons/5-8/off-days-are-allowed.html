Even Clouds Can’t Change the Sun

Feeling grumpy or sad sometimes doesn’t make you a “bad kid” — it just makes you a kid.

Time15-20 min
Groupcircle-time (4-15 kids)
AgeK-2
Formatin-person, needs floor space
UpdatedAugust 19, 2026
Also built for:Grade 3-5Grade 6-8TeenAge 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

Kids this age don’t yet have the words for “I’m having an off day and it doesn’t mean anything’s wrong with me” — but they absolutely know the feeling of being grumpy, sad, or “just not into it” for no reason they can explain. Don’t try to explain the idea to them in a sentence. Let the sun-and-cloud picture carry it. Resist adding a spoken-out-loud “moral” at the end (“so you see, boys and girls, this means...”) — if the story and the drawing worked, they already have it, and naming it out loud often flattens what they just felt. There is no discussion in this lesson on purpose: this age can answer a simple concrete question one at a time, but cannot yet reflect out loud on an abstract idea like “grading yourself,” so we don’t ask them to.

What we're assuming

  • Every child in the room has had a grumpy, sad, or “off” day recently, whether or not they could explain why.
  • This is not the moment to ask what caused it, or to fix the feeling. The point is much narrower: a grumpy day doesn’t make anyone a “bad kid.”
  • Some kids may say something vulnerable mid-story (“I felt like that yesterday”). That’s the lesson working — receive it warmly and keep moving, rather than turning it into a group discussion.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

  1. A felt sense (not a spoken definition) that having a grumpy or sad day sometimes doesn’t make them a “bad kid.”
  2. One picture they can remember: the sun behind a cloud is still the same sun.
  3. A drawing they made themselves, to take home and see again.
  4. The experience of being asked a simple, kind question about their own feelings and having it be okay to answer however they want, including “I don’t know.”

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

  • Keep it moving. This age holds attention for a story and a picture, not for sitting and discussing. If the room gets restless, you’ve probably lingered too long on one beat.
  • Use your voice and face as much as your words — this age reads tone before content.
  • If a child shares something vulnerable, receive it simply (“I’m glad you told me that”) and move on. Don’t turn it into a group conversation.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~3 min

Gathering

Sit in a circle. Welcome warmly: “I’m so glad you’re here today. Every single one of you.” Use names if the group is small enough — this age responds strongly to being named.

Ask (simple, concrete, one at a time — not a discussion): “Has anyone felt grumpy today, even for no reason at all? You don’t have to say why. Just a thumbs up if you have.”

2
~5 min

The story

Tell this simply, in your own words:

“You know how sometimes it’s a bright sunny day, and then a big cloud rolls in front of the sun, and everything gets a little grey and dim for a while? Does the sun stop being the sun? No — it’s still up there, still exactly the same sun, just as warm and just as bright. The cloud doesn’t change it one bit. It just sits in front of it for a while, and then it moves on, and there’s the same sun again.”

Ask: “Has anyone seen that happen — a cloud covering up the sun for a little while?” Let a few kids answer if they want to.

3
~4 min

Naming it as their own grumpy days

“People are kind of like that too. Sometimes you have a grumpy day, or a sad day, or a day where you just don’t feel like yourself — and that’s like a cloud. It doesn’t change who you are underneath. You’re still exactly the same wonderful you, even on a cloudy day.”

Read simply, no need to explain the words:

“Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.”

Psalm 30:5

“That means: a hard feeling doesn’t stay forever. It’s like a cloud — it moves.”

4
~5-6 min

Craft: draw the sun and the cloud

Give each child paper and crayons or markers. Help them draw a big sun, and then a cloud in front of part of it. Under it, help them write or say out loud: “Even on a cloudy day, I’m still me.”

5

Closing

Circle up. Simple closing: “Next time you have a grumpy or sad day, remember — you’re still you underneath, same as always. See you next week.”

What you'll need

The materials

  • Paper and crayons or markers, one per child
  • Nothing else — keep the room simple and uncluttered for this age

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

If a child’s grumpy or sad days sound like more than an ordinary off day — if it comes up again and again, or a child mentions something at home that sounds like real trouble, not just a hard day — don’t probe further in the moment. Stay warm, say something like “thank you for telling me, I care about that,” and follow the site’s mentor safety guidelines (For Mentors page) afterward, including notifying the appropriate adult per your church’s child-safety policy. This age sometimes discloses real concerns gently, inside a story that feels safe — take it seriously even though it arrived quietly.

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
