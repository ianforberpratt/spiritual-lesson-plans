Not Finished Yet (And Still Loved)

You don’t have to finish your picture — or your day — to already be loved, right now.

Time15-20 min
Groupcircle-time (4-15 kids)
AgeK-2
Formatin-person, needs floor space
UpdatedAugust 30, 2026
Also built for:Grade 3-5Grade 6-8TeenAge 21+

Assumptions & Caveats

Before you begin

What you should know

Kids this age think in pictures and stories, not ideas — so this lesson doesn’t explain “permission comes before the fixing,” it lets a child feel it, live, in the middle of something unfinished. The whole lesson turns on one small, real moment: a facilitator noticing and loving something a child is building or drawing before it’s done. Don’t wait to praise the finished thing — that’s the point being made in real time, not just talked about. Resist any urge to add a spoken-out-loud “moral” at the end; if the moment landed, the child already has it. Nothing you don’t already have for prep.

What we're assuming

- Every child in the room has had a moment of not being “done” yet with something — a drawing, a tower, a puzzle, a hard morning — and has felt, even without words for it, some version of “I’m not sure this counts yet.” - This isn’t a lesson about finishing things being bad, or about not trying. It’s much narrower: being loved right now doesn’t wait on a later finish line. - Some kids may want to rush to “done” to avoid the uncomfortable in-between. That’s okay — gently invite them to stay a little longer in “still working on it,” but don’t force it.

Learning goals

What they walk away with

1. A felt sense (not a spoken definition) that being loved doesn’t wait until something is finished. 2. One phrase they can say by heart: “I don’t have to be finished. I’m loved right now.” 3. Something they made themselves — on purpose left a little unfinished — to take home as a reminder.

How to hold the room

Before you start talking

- Keep it moving — a story, a beat of building or drawing, a phrase, a craft. This age’s attention holds for doing, not for sitting and discussing. - The most important moment in this whole lesson is small and easy to rush past: while kids are still mid-build or mid-drawing (Beat 3), stop and warmly notice what’s true right now, not what will be true when they’re done. Don’t skip this by accident. - If a child shares something vulnerable in the moment (“I never finish anything,” “my picture from yesterday got thrown away”), receive it warmly and simply — you don’t need to turn it into a group conversation.

The lesson flow

Step by step

Each beat alternates a little input, a little doing, and a little reflecting. Skip, slow down, or stretch any of these to fit your group.

1
~3 min

Gathering

Sit in a circle. Welcome warmly, using names if the group is small enough: “I’m so glad you’re here today. Just like this — however today’s going for you.”

2
~6 min

The story

Tell simply, in your own words:

“A long time ago, a man named John spent his days by a river, helping people wash off and start fresh. One day, his friend Jesus came and asked for a turn too. John said, ‘Wait — that doesn’t seem right. You should be the one helping me.’ But Jesus said something surprising: ‘It’s okay. Let’s just do this now, together, just as we are.’ So they did. And right then — before Jesus had done one single bit of his big, important work, before anything else happened at all — a voice said: ‘This is the one I love. I’m so pleased with him.’ Not later. Not once he’d finished something big. Right then, in the water, just as he was.”

Ask (simple, one at a time, no pressure): “What do you think it felt like to hear that — right then, before anything else had even happened yet?”

3
~7 min

Building, and being loved partway through

Give kids blocks, or paper and crayons. Let them start building or drawing something. Partway through — while towers are half-built and pictures are half-colored — stop the room gently and go to a few kids, saying something like: “I love this part right here.” “Look at what you’re making — I like it already.” Point out: nobody had to wait until it was all the way done to hear that.

4
~3 min

Say it together

Teach the phrase with a simple motion (open hands, palms up, like receiving something): “I don’t have to be finished. I’m loved right now.” Say it together two or three times.

5
~4 min

Take it home, unfinished on purpose

Have kids stop their tower or drawing while it’s still clearly in progress. Help them write or dictate, on a sticky note or corner of the paper: “Still working on it — and still loved.” Send it home just like that, not finished.

6

Closing

Circle up. Say the phrase together once more: “I don’t have to be finished. I’m loved right now.” “See you next week — just as you are then, too.”

What you'll need

The materials

Wallet card Not Finished Yet For the middle of building something, drawing something, or having a day that isn’t done yet — you’re already loved, right now, exactly as it is. Open printable handout →

- Blocks, or paper and crayons/markers - Sticky notes (optional, for the “still working on it” label) - The “Not Finished Yet” wallet card to send home alongside the craft

If it's not going well

When this one gets heavy

- Silence or shyness during the story questions is normal — let it sit, don’t fill it. - A child wants to rush to “finished” rather than stay in the in-between — gently invite them to pause there a little longer, but don’t force it if they resist. - A strong reaction (a child seems suddenly upset about something unfinished, or about being interrupted) — pause, ground the room with something simple and calm, and check in with that child privately. - A disclosure that sounds like real neglect or harm, not just an ordinary hard day — stay warm, say “thank you for telling me, I care about that,” don’t probe further in the moment, and follow the site’s mentor safety guidelines and your own local mandatory-reporting obligations afterward.

For the fuller tiered guidance — including what to do if someone discloses something serious — see For Mentors.
